# The life of the Porsche 911: a sourced history, 1959–2026

*Research companion to the "Life of a 911" timeline, compiled 27 September 2026. Every paragraph links the pages it draws on. The numbers for each timeline stop are in [`specs.json`](./specs.json), where each field lists its own sources and any disagreements between them.*

## Overview

Porsche first showed the car at the Frankfurt IAA on 12 September 1963, as the **901**. It was a 2+2 fastback designed by Ferdinand Alexander "Butzi" Porsche, with a new air-cooled six-cylinder boxer engine of 2.0 litres and 130 PS mounted in the tail. Series production began in Zuffenhausen on 14 September 1964. Peugeot held a trademark on three-digit numbers with a zero in the middle, so the car was renamed **911** around its market launch ([Porsche 60-years press kit, gen 1](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/1.-Generation-Porsche-911-(Ur-Elfer),-1963-1973.html); [porsche.com](https://www.porsche.com/stories/innovation/a-brief-history-of-the-porsche-911/)). Six decades and eight generations later the basic layout is unchanged: a flat-six behind the rear axle and a sloping fastback roof. Porsche's own drivetrain history sums up the change as "four times the power from twice the displacement" ([Porsche Newsroom, 2024](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2024/history/porsche-drive-system-911-36059.html)). The one-millionth 911 left the line on 11 May 2017 ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-13733.html)). By May 2023 about 1.2 million had been built ([porsche.com](https://www.porsche.com/stories/innovation/a-brief-history-of-the-porsche-911/)), and in 2025 the 911 set a delivery record, with 51,583 cars delivered ([Porsche Newsroom, 2026](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2026/company/porsche-deliveries-2025-41516.html)).

## How this was researched, and how to read the numbers

- **Source hierarchy.** Where sources disagree, the order of trust is: factory figures from press kits, technical-data sheets and Porsche's model pages; then Porsche Newsroom and Porsche Museum history articles; then reputable magazines and registries (Excellence, Total 911, Hagerty, auto motor und sport, Car and Driver, Motor Trend, stuttcars, the PCA); then Wikipedia, used mainly to reach its references. Every disagreement found is recorded in the `discrepancies` array of the relevant stop in `specs.json`. The main backbone source is Porsche's own [60 Years of the 911 press kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911.html).
- **Power.** `power_ps` is the German DIN/ECE figure. `power_hp` is an official US horsepower figure from Porsche Cars North America where one was found; otherwise it is PS × 0.98632, rounded, and the stop's `notes` say which. Period US-market SAE ratings differ and are noted where found. For example, Porsche Cars North America quotes 388 hp for the 394 PS 992.2 Carrera ([US press kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:928354fb-31e2-4b4c-af93-2c22d6de07b4/US%20911%20Carrera%20and%20Carrera%20GTS%20Press%20Kit.pdf)).
- **Weight.** DIN unladen weight (full tank, no driver) is the primary figure. The EU (EC-directive) figure adds a driver and luggage. Porsche, for example, listed the 997 Carrera at 1,395 kg DIN and 1,470 kg under the EC directive ([porsche.com, 2008](https://web.archive.org/web/20080402125856/http://www.porsche.com:80/international/models/911/911-carrera/featuresandspecs/)). The EU figure is given as an alternative where Porsche published one.
- **Years.** "Production years" are calendar build years. "Model years" (MY) begin after the summer factory break, so a car built in autumn 1973 is MY1974 ([Porsche 60-years kit, gen 2](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/2.-Generation-911,-(G-Serie),-1973---1989.html)). Porsche identifies model years internally by letter. The first cars, built until July 1967, are known as the O series. MY1968 was the A series and MY1969 the B series ([en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))). This first sequence ran to the M series (MY1979), skipping the letter I. From MY1980 the letters restarted as "programmes", A-Programm, and continued to the Y-Programm of MY2000 ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))). MY1974 was the G series, and the name stuck to the whole second generation. Its last cars, MY1989, were the second-sequence K-Programm ([Porsche, 50 years of the G-Series](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html); [Porsche 60-years kit, gen 2](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/2.-Generation-911,-(G-Serie),-1973---1989.html)).
- **Production counts.** Porsche has published several different totals for the same generation. This document and `specs.json` use the figures from the 2023 "60 years" press kit for every generation so they can be compared; the alternatives are listed in the production section below. The Porsche figures come from the [60 Years of the 911 press kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911.html) and [porsche.com](https://www.porsche.com/stories/innovation/a-brief-history-of-the-porsche-911/).

## Who shaped it: the 911's heads of design

Porsche itself counts only five heads of design in its history. When it announced Michael Mauer's successor in 2026 it wrote that Mauer "started in 2004 as the fourth Head of Design in Porsche's long history. His predecessors were Ferdinand Alexander Porsche, Anatol Lapine and Harm Lagaay." Tobias Sühlmann (46, previously McLaren's Chief Design Officer) took over on 1 February 2026, and Mauer stays on to support him during a transition ([Porsche Newsroom, 2026](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2026/company/porsche-head-of-design-tobias-suehlmann-michael-mauer-41611.html)). ## Production milestones

- **1 December 1963:** Porsche buys the Reutter body works, with about 1,000 employees, and secures its Zuffenhausen site. Every 911 has since been built in Zuffenhausen, and all variants share a single line ([Porsche 2025](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en_SG/2025/history/porsche-75-years-zuffenhausen-production-anniversary-39059.html)).
- **Summer 1987, the 250,000th 911:** marked by an anniversary model nicknamed the "quarter millionaire". It was painted Diamond Blue Metallic and had Ferry Porsche's signature on the headrests. 875 were built: 505 Coupés, 165 Targas and 205 Cabriolets ([Porsche 2024](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2024/history/porsche-klassik-911-g-model-anniversary-model-1987-35245.html)).
- **11 May 2017, the 1,000,000th 911:** a 991.2 Carrera S in Irish Green with Pepita seat centres and a mahogany wheel, built as a Porsche Exclusive one-off and kept by Porsche. The colour recalls Ferry Porsche's own green 911, chassis 300.003, handed over on 19 October 1964 ([press release](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-13733.html), [details](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-details-13734.html)). Porsche said at the time that more than 70 % of all 911s ever built were still roadworthy ([press release](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-13733.html)).
- No primary source was found for a 100,000th or 500,000th 911 celebration.

## 00-t7-1959 — The 356 successor programme and the Type 754 "T7" (1957–1962)

### Why the 356 had to be replaced
By the late 1950s the Porsche 356 "was approaching the limits of its capabilities": created as a Volkswagen derivative, "the potential of the engine, chassis and bodywork had been virtually exhausted" ([Porsche Newsroom – From the 754 via the 901 to the 911](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html)). Almost 78,000 356s were sold, and the successor had to continue that success while being bigger – room for a golf bag in the luggage compartment and a 2+2 interior ([Porsche Newsroom, 1-millionth-911 press kit 2017](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-automotive-icon-sports-car-13737.html)). Ferry Porsche's own wish-list, jotted on squared paper, read: "2-seater with 2 comfortable jump seats. Rear view mirror integrated in the wings. Easier entry", while the sales department demanded: "Retain previous Porsche line. Not a fundamentally new car. Sporty character" ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html)). Another Porsche text summarises his brief as "more interior space, more output, lower production costs. And yet it was supposed to be a true Porsche" ([Porsche Newsroom – "I Am Legend"](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-construction-sketch-10955.html)).

### Competing proposals
Porsche had no design department at the time – styling was part of body development – and "several proposals from the engineers and from well-known designers" were rejected by Ferry Porsche because, "as nice as they were ... they were not a Porsche" ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html)). AutoBild Klassik names the losers: veteran body engineer Erwin Komenda (who had shaped the 356), in-house stylist Heinrich Klie and BMW 507 designer Albrecht Graf Goertz, all with four-seat ideas ([AutoBild Klassik, 2014](https://www.autobild.de/klassik/artikel/porsche-754-t7-gluecklose-prototypen-teil-4-von-4-5236879.html)). kfz-tech.de says Goertz's plasticine study (numbered 695 there) was judged "too American" ([kfz-tech.de](https://www.kfz-tech.de/Hersteller/Porsche/Porsche7541959.htm)). A four-seat 356 had been tried before, as the Type 530 of the early 1950s ([Porsche Newsroom – Four wins](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2019/history/porsche-four-wins-sports-car-four-seats-18908.html)). In parallel Komenda ran the T6 body project – a 356 B facelift with bigger lids and rear window that reached production in 1962 ([Wikipedia EN – Porsche 911 (classic)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))).

### Butzi Porsche and the T7
Ferry's eldest son, Ferdinand Alexander "Butzi" Porsche (born 11 December 1935), joined the design office in 1957 according to two Porsche texts ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html); [Porsche Newsroom, F. A. Porsche at 90](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2025/history/porsche-ferdinand-alexander-90-years-41290.html)) – or in 1958 according to Porsche's 2012 obituary and the 2017 press kit ([Excellence, reproducing Porsche AG's obituary](https://www.excellence-mag.com/of-note/f-a-butzi-porsche-1935-2012); [Porsche Newsroom 2017](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-automotive-icon-sports-car-13737.html)). In 1959 he sculpted a plasticine model of a 356 successor, and the 754 "T7" prototype "was created on the basis of his designs ... an extremely promising study of a four-seater car, however its rear end did not yet meet with Ferry Porsche's approval" ([Porsche Newsroom 2017](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-automotive-icon-sports-car-13737.html)). Car and Driver dates the full-size mock-up to "the waning days of 1959" ([Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/butzis-rough-draft-the-porsche-t7-concept-was-the-911s-direct-forefather)); another Porsche article says the running prototype 754 followed in 1960 ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html)), and AutoBild says it went into testing on 1 November 1960 ([AutoBild Klassik](https://www.autobild.de/klassik/artikel/porsche-754-t7-gluecklose-prototypen-teil-4-von-4-5236879.html)). Wikipedia and AutoBild label the car "1961", and a Porsche photo caption even says "1961/62" – probably because the car kept changing ([Wikipedia EN – Typ 754 T7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_Typ_754_T7); [Porsche Newsroom DE](https://newsroom.porsche.com/de/historie/porsche-911-entstehungsgeschichte-754-901-ferry-porsche-14642.html)). Inside the factory it was simply the "Butzi-Wagen" ([AutoBild Klassik](https://www.autobild.de/klassik/artikel/porsche-754-t7-gluecklose-prototypen-teil-4-von-4-5236879.html)).

### What it looked like
Porsche says the 754 already had "most of the typical characteristics" of the 911: "the flattened bonnet between the free-standing wings. The inclined built-in headlights, the A-pillars with the windscreen, the elegantly shaped rear section" ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html)). Its wheelbase was 2.40 m ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html)) against the 356's 2,100 mm ([2013 book excerpt on the IAA 1963](https://silo.tips/download/die-porsche-911-premiere-das-highlight-auf-der-iaa-in-frankfurt-1963)); AutoBild gives 4,340 × 1,730 × 1,350 mm and 165/80 R 15 tyres ([AutoBild Klassik](https://www.autobild.de/klassik/artikel/porsche-754-t7-gluecklose-prototypen-teil-4-von-4-5236879.html)). In profile it is a notchback whose wraparound rear glass makes it look like a fastback ([Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/butzis-rough-draft-the-porsche-t7-concept-was-the-911s-direct-forefather)); Motor1 notes a chrome element across the nose, 356-style round headlamps, a flat tail with horizontal lamps and two central exhaust outlets ([Motor1](https://www.motor1.com/news/690730/porsche-type-754-concept-history/)). Porsche archive photos show an early full-size model with quad headlamps and the car first carrying a "1600 SUPER" script; it was later fitted with the Carrera engine and painted metallic green ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-f-series-the-story/)).

### Engines tested
The T7 was built with a four-speed transaxle ([Excellence](https://www.excellence-mag.com/issues/304/articles/out-of-the-shadows)). Its first new engine, the Type 745 – a 2.0-litre pushrod flat-six with twin axial fans – proved a "low-revving dead end", and test chief Helmuth Bott's verdict was "Das können wir vergessen!" ("We can forget that!") ([AutoBild Klassik](https://www.autobild.de/klassik/artikel/porsche-754-t7-gluecklose-prototypen-teil-4-von-4-5236879.html)). Car and Driver reports only 120 hp from it, more once bored out to 2.2 litres, and that Ferry Porsche banned further pushrod development after driving it ([Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/butzis-rough-draft-the-porsche-t7-concept-was-the-911s-direct-forefather)). The car ended up with the 130 PS four-cam Carrera 2 engine (1,966 cc, 162 Nm), good for about 200 km/h ([AutoBild Klassik](https://www.autobild.de/klassik/artikel/porsche-754-t7-gluecklose-prototypen-teil-4-von-4-5236879.html); displacement matches the [Porsche Museum's 356 B 2000 GS](https://web.archive.org/web/2021id_/https://presskit.porsche.de/museum/en/2019/topic/exhibitions/cars/id-356-b-2000-gs-carrera-gt-1960.html)). That engine was too complex, noisy and expensive for series production ([Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/butzis-rough-draft-the-porsche-t7-concept-was-the-911s-direct-forefather)). Wikipedia instead says the car was first made drivable with a 1600 Super pushrod four as "Typ 695", and only in 1962–63 got the Carrera GS engine and the number 754 ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))).

### From T7 to T8 and 901
Ferry Porsche insisted on a fastback and rejected the four-seater; as a compromise the 2.40 m wheelbase was cut to 2.20 m ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html)). kfz-tech.de quotes him: "Schuster bleib bei deinen Leisten. Viersitzer gibt es genug." ("Cobbler, stick to your last. There are enough four-seaters.") ([kfz-tech.de](https://www.kfz-tech.de/Hersteller/Porsche/Porsche7541959.htm)). The T8 (2+2) and a T9 full four-seater were developed in parallel, and Ferry personally approved further development of the T8 in 1961 – "in effect ... the birth of the 911" ([Porsche Newsroom – Four wins](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2019/history/porsche-four-wins-sports-car-four-seats-18908.html)). stuttcars says Butzi presented the final T8 design in December 1961 ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-f-series-the-story/)), and Porsche says the T8 was developed from 1962 under the project name "Type 901" ([Porsche Newsroom 2017](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-automotive-icon-sports-car-13737.html)). Butzi became head of the design studio in 1961 (Porsche 2025) or 1962 (Porsche 2012 obituary) ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2025/history/porsche-ferdinand-alexander-90-years-41290.html); [Excellence](https://www.excellence-mag.com/of-note/f-a-butzi-porsche-1935-2012)).

### The car today
The single T7 survives and is displayed at the Porsche Museum in Stuttgart ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_Typ_754_T7); [Motor1](https://www.motor1.com/news/690730/porsche-type-754-concept-history/)). Wikimedia Commons has about 22 photos of it from 2009–2013 (front, rear, side and overhead views, including Rétromobile 2013) ([Commons search](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?search=Porsche+754+T7&title=Special:MediaSearch&type=image)), and stuttcars reproduces Porsche archive shots of its earlier states ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-f-series-the-story/)). That is enough reference material for a 3D model.

## 01-901-1963 — The Porsche 901: prototypes, IAA 1963 premiere, and the renaming to 911 (1962–1964)

### Development of the 901 (1962–1963)
Work on the 2+2 fastback started in 1962 under the type number 901; the first prototype rolled out of the Zuffenhausen gates for a test drive on the evening of 9 November 1962 ([Porsche Newsroom – From the 754 via the 901 to the 911](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html)). The number came from Volkswagen's spare-part number ranges, because a collaboration with VW was expected and the 900 numbers were free in Wolfsburg: 901 for the six-cylinder car and 902 for a later four-cylinder version ([Porsche Newsroom 2017](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-automotive-icon-sports-car-13737.html)). Excellence counts ten 901 prototypes built between late 1962 and 1964 on "replacement" chassis numbers starting at 13 321, plus two 902/912 test cars and one cabriolet ([Excellence – Out of the Shadows](https://www.excellence-mag.com/issues/304/articles/out-of-the-shadows)); German Wikipedia says 13 prototypes were built as 901 ([Wikipedia DE – Porsche 911 (1963)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))). The prototypes had nicknames – "Sturmvogel" (13 321, white, first run with a 356 Super 90 engine), "Fledermaus", "Blaumeise", "Zitronenfalter", "Quickblau" and "Barbarossa" ([Excellence](https://www.excellence-mag.com/issues/304/articles/out-of-the-shadows)). Porsche was still also a busy engineering consultancy, and because body supplier Reutter would not fund the new tooling, Porsche took over its pressing plant and about 1,000 employees in summer 1963 ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html)).

### Design and engineering team
F. A. "Butzi" Porsche is credited by Porsche as the designer of "the 2+2 seater with a hatchback on a unibody frame" ([Porsche Newsroom – The idea Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/Die-Idee-Porsche.html)). Its signature elements were "the distinctive headlight 'tunnels', the flat bonnet between them, the hatchback with a large rear window and the short overhangs" ([Porsche 60 Years press kit – 1st gen design](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/1.-Generation-Design-und-Modellvarianten.html)). A 1963 studio photo shows him with Heinrich Klie, Ernst Bolt, Hans Ploch and Hans Springmann ([Porsche Newsroom 2025](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2025/history/porsche-ferdinand-alexander-90-years-41290.html)). Erwin Komenda, head of body construction, engineered the new body and chassis around the new engine, gearbox, steering and suspension ([Wikipedia EN – 911 (classic)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))). German Wikipedia goes further and describes the shape as Butzi's work "together with Erwin Komenda" ([Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))). For the engine, Porsche says Ferdinand Piëch "oversaw the development" of the air-cooled flat-six, with chain-driven overhead camshafts, 2.0 litres to start and room to grow to 2.7 litres ([Porsche Newsroom 2017](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-automotive-icon-sports-car-13737.html)). Porsche's obituary of Hans Mezger lists "1963 Design of the 901/911 engine" ([Porsche Newsroom 2020](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2020/company/porsche-mourns-death-of-hans-mezger-21199.html)). One enthusiast history names Hans Tomala as responsible for engine development, with Piëch joining "at the final stage" ([autozine](https://www.autozine.org/911/911_3.htm)). According to Wikipedia, the path to the final engine went from the twin-fan pushrod Type 745 through a wet-sump overhead-cam Type 821 to the dry-sump 901, after Piëch argued for dry-sump lubrication ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic)); [Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/butzis-rough-draft-the-porsche-t7-concept-was-the-911s-direct-forefather)).

### The finished engine and chassis
The flat-six had eight main bearings, dry-sump lubrication and one camshaft per head driven by intermediate shafts and a chain. With 9:1 compression it made 130 PS from 1,991 cc ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html)), with bore × stroke of 80 × 66 mm and 174 Nm at 4,200 rpm ([Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))). The engine speed for peak power is given as 6,200 rpm by Porsche's heritage article but 6,100 rpm in other data – see the JSON discrepancies. A new five-speed gearbox, shared with the 904 Carrera GTS, transmitted the power ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html)). The chassis replaced the 356's swing axle with semi-trailing arms at the rear, and used MacPherson-strut front suspension with longitudinal torsion bars to free up luggage space ([2013 book excerpt](https://silo.tips/download/die-porsche-911-premiere-das-highlight-auf-der-iaa-in-frankfurt-1963); [Porsche Newsroom 2017](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-automotive-icon-sports-car-13737.html)). The 901 was about 120 mm longer and 70 mm narrower than the 356 C and about 100 kg heavier ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-f-series-the-story/)).

### World premiere: IAA Frankfurt, 12 September 1963
Porsche celebrated the world premiere "at the IAA on September 12 ... with a pre-series vehicle, even though its specification was far from being finalised" ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html)). The stand was in Hall 1a (stand 27, 211 m²). The car shown was the fifth prototype, chassis 13 325, with a Karmann-built body painted yellow. It had a dummy six-cylinder, which was swapped for a running engine at the end of 1963, and it lacked the production car's bumper overriders, sill trim and slanted gold script, and had twin tailpipes ([2013 book excerpt](https://silo.tips/download/die-porsche-911-premiere-das-highlight-auf-der-iaa-in-frankfurt-1963); [Excellence](https://www.excellence-mag.com/issues/304/articles/out-of-the-shadows)). Wikipedia instead says the working 901 engine arrived in February 1964 ([Wikipedia EN – Porsche 911](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911)). The announced price was 23,900 DM, including leather trim that was not even an option at production start, and orders were signed without test drives or delivery dates ([book excerpt](https://silo.tips/download/die-porsche-911-premiere-das-highlight-auf-der-iaa-in-frankfurt-1963)). ADAC Motorwelt's 1 October 1963 price list also shows the 901 at 23,900 DM, above the 356 Carrera 2 at 23,700 DM ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-f-series-the-story/)). The "official 901 specs in September 1963" were 4,135 × 1,600 × 1,273 mm, a 2,204 mm wheelbase, 4.5 × 15 wheels with 165 R 15 tyres, 210 km/h and 0–100 km/h in 9.1 s ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-f-series-the-story/)). Development continued until May 1964, when the car could finally be presented to industry professionals ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html)).

### Production, Peugeot and the rename
Series production of the 901 began on 14 September 1964, and the base price was set at 21,900 DM ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html); [Porsche 60 Years press kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/1.-Generation-Porsche-911-(Ur-Elfer),-1963-1973.html)). When a car was shown at the October 1964 Paris Motor Show, Peugeot objected ([porsche.com Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-f/911-f-901/)). Peugeot said it had used three-digit numbers with a zero in the middle since 1929 and held the rights to all such numbers in France ([Porsche Newsroom 2017](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-automotive-icon-sports-car-13737.html)). Porsche rejected alternatives such as adding "GT" and simply doubled the existing "1" font, because brochures, price lists, manuals and badges were already made ([Porsche Newsroom 2017](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-automotive-icon-sports-car-13737.html)). Porsche's own date for Ferry Porsche's rename order is inconsistent: **22 October 1964** in the museum's "No. 57" history ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html)), but **22 November 1964** in the 2017 press kit ([Porsche Newsroom 2017](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-automotive-icon-sports-car-13737.html)). In total **82 cars were built as 901s** ([porsche.com Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-f/911-f-901/)), between 14 September and 16 November 1964 according to auto motor und sport via Wikipedia ([Wikipedia EN – Porsche 901](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_901)). Customer cars from that period "were manufactured as 901 vehicles, but sold as 911 vehicles" ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-museum-special-exhibition-oldest-911-901-number-57-restoration-14644.html)). The part numbers kept the 901 prefix for years ([Wikipedia EN – Porsche 911](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911)), and Porsche treats the whole first generation (1963–1973) as the original 911 or "Typ 901" ([Porsche 60 Years press kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/1.-Generation-Porsche-911-(Ur-Elfer),-1963-1973.html); [Wikipedia DE – Porsche 901](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_901)). The racing 904 and 906 were likewise sold as "Carrera GTS" and "Carrera 6" ([Wikipedia EN – Porsche 901](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_901)).

### The four-cylinder 912
To close the price gap to the 356, the four-cylinder 912 was launched in April 1965. It used the 911 body with the 356 SC's 1.6-litre flat-four, detuned from 95 to 90 PS, and a four-speed gearbox ([porsche.com Classic – 912](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-f/911-f-912/); [porsche.com Classic – 911 F](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-f/)).

### "No. 57" – the museum's oldest 911
Chassis 300 057, in Signal Red 6407, was built in October 1964 as a 901 ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-museum-restoration-number-57-911-901-barn-find-collection-14639.html)). Porsche's heritage article says it was the third and last car built on 22 October 1964, the day of the rename order ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html)). On 5 August 2014 an RTL2 docu-soap crew – the show was *Der Trödeltrupp* ([Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_901)) – reported it in a barn on a former farm in Brandenburg. Porsche paid €107,000 for it and €14,500 for a 1968 911 L found alongside. The three-year restoration reused genuine 1965 body panels ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-museum-restoration-number-57-911-901-barn-find-collection-14639.html)), and the car was first shown in the special exhibition "911 (901 No. 57) – A legend takes off" from 14 December 2017 to 8 April 2018 ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-museum-special-exhibition-oldest-911-901-number-57-restoration-14644.html)). The oldest known surviving 901 prototype, 13 326 "Quickblau", once belonged to Piëch and then Mezger and has been restored by Alois Ruf ([Excellence](https://www.excellence-mag.com/issues/304/articles/out-of-the-shadows)).

## 02-911-swb-1964 — 911 2.0 (O- and A-series, short wheelbase), 1964–1968

### From show car to production car
Porsche unveiled the successor to the 356 as the "901" at the IAA in Frankfurt on 12 September 1963 ([Porsche Newsroom – seven generations, part 1](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-1-original-911-16452.html)). Series production of the 901 began on **14 September 1964**. The base price was **21,900 DM**, and the first air-cooled six-cylinder boxer produced **130 PS**, good for **210 km/h** ([Porsche 60-years press kit, 1st generation](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/1.-Generation-Porsche-911-(Ur-Elfer),-1963-1973.html); [Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-f/911-f-911/)). The name changed within weeks. Peugeot claimed the French rights to every three-digit name with a zero in the middle, and Porsche had already made the "901" badges, posters and brochures. Chief designer Ferdinand Alexander Porsche "simply doubles the '1'" ([Porsche Newsroom – "The middle zero moment"](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-history-901-911-iaa-12274.html)). According to the Porsche Museum, every customer car built up to that point was manufactured as a 901 but sold as a 911 ([Newsroom – The return of the Number 57](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-museum-restoration-number-57-911-901-barn-find-collection-14639.html)). "901" remained the type number of the engine and gearbox and the parts-number prefix until the 1970 C-series ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))). Stuttcars dates the start of production to August 1964, but Porsche's own figure is 14 September ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-1964-1968/)). The 911 and 356 were built side by side from September 1964 to March 1965 ([Wikipedia DE – Porsche 912](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_912)).

### Who designed it
Porsche credits the shape to **Ferdinand Alexander "Butzi" Porsche**. His signatures include the headlamp "tunnels" with a flat bonnet between them, a fastback with a large rear window, and short overhangs ([Porsche press kit – design](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/1.-Generation-Design-und-Modellvarianten.html)). **Erwin Komenda**, head of body construction, engineered the body and chassis ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic)); [Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))). Porsche calls **Hans Mezger** the designer of the air-cooled six-cylinder boxer ([Newsroom – The creative spirit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2020/history/porsche-911-hans-mezger-creative-spirit-18921.html)). Wikipedia EN adds that Klaus von Rücker and Leopold Jäntschke led the engine team and that Ferdinand Piëch pushed for dry-sump lubrication ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))). F. A. Porsche also gave the later Fuchs wheel its "cloverleaf" spoke shape: Fuchs presented its first design in May 1965, and "Mr. Porsche Jr." restyled it ([Christophorus via Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/christophorus/porsche-christophorus-911-fuchs-rim-ferdinand-porsche-performance-wheels-13017.html)).

### The car
- **Engine:** 1,991 cc (80 × 66 mm) air-cooled flat-six with one chain-driven overhead camshaft per bank and dry-sump lubrication. It made 96 kW (130 PS) at 6,100 rpm and 174 Nm at 4,200 rpm on 9.0:1 compression, fed by two Solex triple-choke carburettors that were replaced by Webers from 1966 ([Wikipedia DE tech table](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963)); [Excellence specs 1965 911](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/9); [Porsche infographic](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:93042c81-579d-4a9f-b005-3f6ae9e1718c/01_911er_Generationen_Ur-Elfer_EN.jpg)). In the US, Porsche of America advertised it at 148 hp SAE ([DerWhite's literature archive](https://derwhites356literature.com/DerWhites911912SalesLiterature.html)).
- **Gearbox:** 5-speed Type 901 transaxle with a dog-leg first gear ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))).
- **Factory performance and weight:** 0–100 km/h in 9.1 s, 210 km/h, 1,080 kg ([Porsche data sheet, 911 2.0](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:a76c5e2e-815a-4aba-940b-9bfac8ca7120/Datenblatt%20Porsche%20911%202.0%20Targa.pdf)).
- **Size:** 4,163 × 1,610 × 1,320 mm on a 2,211 mm wheelbase. Early tracks were 1,337/1,317 mm, widening to 1,367/1,335 mm for MY1967 ([Excellence 1965](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/9), [1967](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/11); [auto-data](https://www.auto-data.net/en/porsche-911-coupe-f-2.0-130hp-6632)).
- **Wheels and tyres:** 165 HR 15 tyres on 4½J steel wheels with chrome hubcaps ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-1964-1968/)).
- **Compared with the 356:** a wheelbase about 11 cm longer, more glass, a bigger front boot, rack-and-pinion steering with a twice-angled safety column, and MacPherson struts at the front with semi-trailing arms at the rear, all sprung by torsion bars ([Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).

### Model-year evolution (O-series → A-series)
- **1965 (MY1965):** Porsche added the 4-cylinder **912** (90 PS, 16,250 DM), a cheaper 911 for 356 customers ([Wikipedia DE – 912](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_912); [Porsche infographic](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:93042c81-579d-4a9f-b005-3f6ae9e1718c/01_911er_Generationen_Ur-Elfer_EN.jpg)). From September 1965 the gold "911" script sat straight and centred instead of slanted ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-f/911-f-911/)).
- **Targa:** shown at the IAA in September 1965 as "the first safety cabriolet in the world", with a roughly 20 cm-wide stainless-steel roll bar, a removable roof and a zip-out plastic "soft window" ([Newsroom – Targa history](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2020/products/porsche-911-targa-generations-history-21010.html); [Porsche infographic](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:93042c81-579d-4a9f-b005-3f6ae9e1718c/01_911er_Generationen_Ur-Elfer_EN.jpg)). "911 Flori" was considered before sales chief Harald Wagner suggested simply "Targa". German autobahn police took the first cars in December 1966, and public sales began in January 1967. A fixed glass rear window became optional for MY1968 and standard for MY1969 ([Porsche data sheet](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:a76c5e2e-815a-4aba-940b-9bfac8ca7120/Datenblatt%20Porsche%20911%202.0%20Targa.pdf); [Newsroom – Targa history](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2020/products/porsche-911-targa-generations-history-21010.html)).
- **1966 (MY1967):** the **911 S** arrived with 160 PS and was the first car on forged **Fuchs** alloys, saving about 3 kg per wheel ([Newsroom – Fuchs](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/christophorus/porsche-christophorus-911-fuchs-rim-ferdinand-porsche-performance-wheels-13017.html); [Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-f/911-f-911/)). From September 1966 the cars got wider sill trims and rubber-padded bumper overriders ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-f/911-f-911/)). To counter the car's tendency to oversteer, two 11 kg cast-iron ballast weights went behind the front bumper from 1966 ([Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).
- **1967 – 911 R:** 19 customer cars and 4 test cars. The 901/22 race engine made 210 PS at 8,000 rpm, the car weighed about 800 kg and ran 0–100 km/h in 5.9 s. The estimated price of 45,000 DM was nearly twice that of a 911 S ([Newsroom – 911 R history](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-r-geneva-motorshow-racing-history-12282.html)).
- **Autumn 1967 (MY1968, A-series):** the range split into the **911 T** (110 PS, 4-speed, silver script), the **911 L** (the 130 PS car renamed "Luxus") and the **911 S** ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-f/911-f-911/); [Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))). The **Sportomatic** semi-automatic became an option ([Porsche press kit – engines](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/1.-Generation---Motoren.html); [press kit – innovations](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/1.-Generation---Innovationen.html)). The wipers now parked on the left ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-f/911-f-911/)). US emissions rules kept the 911 S out of the US for 1968 ([DerWhite's](https://derwhites356literature.com/DerWhites911912SalesLiterature.html)).
- **Motorsport:** in January 1968 Vic Elford and David Stone won the Monte Carlo Rally in a 911 T, Porsche's first Monte win, with Pauli Toivonen second ([Christophorus via Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/christophorus/issue-382/porsche-911-rally-monte-carlo-1968-vic-elford-13947.html)).

## 03-911-lwb-1969 — 911 long wheelbase (B, C, D, E, F series), MY1969–1973

### Why the wheelbase grew
The early 911 was criticised for pronounced oversteer. From 1966 Porsche even fitted two 11 kg cast-iron ballast weights behind the front bumper ([Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))). For model year 1969, the B-series built from mid-1968, the wheelbase grew by **57 mm to 2,268 mm**, which "primarily … gave the rear-engined sports car calmer handling" ([Porsche Newsroom – seven generations, part 1](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-1-original-911-16452.html); [press kit – design](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/1.-Generation-Design-und-Modellvarianten.html)). Porsche Classic dates the change "from September 1968" ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-f/911-f-911/)). Stuttcars says the A-series ended in August 1968 ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-t-2-0-1968-1969/)).

The body was not lengthened. The rear wheels moved back on longer semi-trailing arms, the wheel openings were adapted, the front ballast was deleted, and the battery was split into two smaller units, one in each front wheel-arch area ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic)); [Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))). Stuttcars notes "flared wheelwells to accommodate larger tires", with 5.5-inch wheels now standard on the 911 T ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-t-2-0-1968-1969/)).

### The three-model range: T, E, S (MY1969)
- **911 T** (Touring): the entry coupé and the reference for this stop. Weber carburettors, 1,991 cc, 81 kW (110 PS) at 5,800 rpm, 157 Nm at 4,200 rpm, 8.6:1 compression. It came with a 4-speed gearbox as standard, with a 5-speed or Sportomatic optional ([Wikipedia DE tech table](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963)); [Excellence 1969 911 T](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/18)).
  - Performance: about 10 s to 100 km/h and 200 km/h (124 mph) ([Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963)); [Excellence](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/18)).
  - Weight: 1,020 kg per Excellence (2,249 lb) and a Porsche Newsroom UK feature, which also quotes a UK price of £2,110. DE Wikipedia lists 1,080 kg ([Porsche Newsroom – "The 911 T that slept for 10 years"](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2019/history/porsche-911-carrera-t-1969-car-park-18794.html)).
  - Dimensions: 4,163 × 1,610 × 1,320 mm, tracks 1,362/1,343 mm, 165 HR 15 tyres on 5½J rims, Cd 0.38 ([Excellence](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/18); [Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).
- **911 E:** new for MY1969 and replacing the 911 L. Bosch/Kugelfischer mechanical fuel injection gave 140 PS; "E" stood for Einspritzung (injection) ([Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-1-original-911-16452.html); [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))). Self-levelling hydropneumatic front struts were fitted for comfort ([Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).
- **911 S:** fuel-injected, 170 PS at 6,800 rpm, on 6J × 15 wheels with 185/70 VR 15 tyres ([Excellence 1969 911 S](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/25); [Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).
- **Engine and body:** die-cast magnesium engine parts cut 16 kg ([Wikipedia DE, citing KFT 11/1968](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))). From 1969 a sealed body cavity protected against rust ([Porsche press kit – innovations](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/1.-Generation---Innovationen.html)). The Targa's fixed, heated glass rear window became standard for MY1969 ([Porsche data sheet](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:a76c5e2e-815a-4aba-940b-9bfac8ca7120/Datenblatt%20Porsche%20911%202.0%20Targa.pdf)).
- **Emissions:** Porsche says that with the T, E and S it became the first German manufacturer to meet the strict US EPA emissions standards ([press kit – engines](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/1.-Generation---Motoren.html)).

### 2.2 litres (C/D series, MY1970–71)
- **Engine:** the bore went from 80 to 84 mm (2,195 cc). Power rose to **125 PS (T), 155 PS (E) and 180 PS (S)**, and every crankcase was now cast in magnesium ([Porsche data sheet 911 S 2.2](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:a7ebe72e-5657-48ae-b41f-dfccf7f4242d/Datenblatt%20Porsche%20911%20S%202.2%20Targa.pdf); [radical-mag](https://radical-mag.com/2017/05/23/porsche-911-t-targa-1970/)).
- **911 S 2.2 Targa (Porsche data sheet):** 132 kW, 230 km/h, 0–100 km/h in 7.5 s, 1,020 kg ([data sheet](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:a7ebe72e-5657-48ae-b41f-dfccf7f4242d/Datenblatt%20Porsche%20911%20S%202.2%20Targa.pdf)).
- **Targa share:** almost a third of 1970 production was Targas ([data sheet](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:a7ebe72e-5657-48ae-b41f-dfccf7f4242d/Datenblatt%20Porsche%20911%20S%202.2%20Targa.pdf); [Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/Porsche-Heritage-Experience/Porsche-911-S-2.2-Targa.html)).
- **C-series details:** the "911" parts prefix replaced "901", the clutch grew larger, the engine lid was aluminium, and door handles gained a concealed pull lever. CDI ignition and ventilated discs became standard ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic)); [Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-f/911-f-911/)).
- **D-series (MY1971):** hot-dip zinc underbody protection and door side-impact beams ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))).
- **Prices:** a 1970 911 S 2.2 Targa cost 31,080 DM ([Newsroom – For eternity](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2019/history/porsche-klassik-911-eight-generations-dna-18914.html)). Sources conflict on the 911 T 2.2 Targa: 23,199 DM per *auto motor und sport* 8/1970 as cited by [Wikipedia DE – Porsche 914](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_914), versus 21,911 Mark per [radical-mag](https://radical-mag.com/2017/05/23/porsche-911-t-targa-1970/).
- **911 S/T ("ST"):** a racing package offered as a road-legal "Sport Standard Version" (M471) from October 1970, plus M491 (circuit) and M494 (rally). It won the 1970 Monte Carlo Rally 1-2-4, and its 2.5-litre final evolution made 270 PS ([Newsroom – History 911 S (ST)](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/911-s-t/History-911-S-ST--1969-to-1972.html)). Porsche had also won the Monte Carlo Rally in 1968 with a 911 T and again in 1969 ([Newsroom – Monte Carlo](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-monte-carlo-rally-1969-double-victory-911-t-col-de-turini-herbert-linge-vic-elford-rene-rochebrun-jean-pierre-nicolas-jaques-almeras-15695.html)).

### 2.4 litres (E/F series, MY1972–73)
- **Engine:** the stroke went to 70.4 mm (2,341 cc, marketed as "2.4"), and the engines ran on regular-grade petrol ([Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-1-original-911-16452.html)). Outputs:
  - **911 T:** 130 PS with Zenith carburettors, or 140 PS with injection in the USA and Japan. The US T switched to Bosch K-Jetronic from January 1973 ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))).
  - **911 E:** 165 PS.
  - **911 S:** 190 PS, the fastest German production car of 1972 at 230 km/h ([Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).
- **Gearbox:** a stronger Type 915 transmission replaced the 901 and dropped the dog-leg first gear ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))).
- **Oil-filler flap:** for MY1972 only, the oil tank moved ahead of the right rear wheel, with a filler flap on the right rear wing. It was dropped after a year, reputedly because attendants filled it with petrol. Just under 10,000 of these "Ölklappe" cars were built ([Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963)); [Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))).
- **911 S front spoiler:** the S gained a discreet spoiler under the front bumper ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))).
- **Trim changes:** 2.4 cars have a black engine-lid grille with a chrome "2.4" badge and black anodised lettering. MY1973 added black horn grilles and rectangular door mirrors ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-f/911-f-911/)). Wikipedia EN attributes the black lettering and grilles to the F-series (MY1973), after Pininfarina's 1969 B17 study ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))).
- **Wheelbase:** 2.4 cars are listed at 2,271 mm ([Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963)); [Excellence 1972 911 T](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/21)).
- **Carrera RS 2.7 (MY1973):** closes this era and has its own stop (04). Porsche's press kit notes it was the first production car with integrated front and rear spoilers ([press kit – design](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/1.-Generation-Design-und-Modellvarianten.html)).

### Designers

## 04-carrera-rs-27-1973 — 911 Carrera RS 2.7 (1972–1973)

### Why it was built
By 1972 Porsche's racing effort had moved from the outlawed 917 prototypes to GT racing with the 911, and the fastest road 911 (the 2.4 S) was no longer enough. Porsche began developing the Carrera RS 2.7 in May 1972 as a homologation special — "It was to be a very light, fast sports car," recalled Peter Falk, then Porsche's Head of Testing for series-production cars — with around 15 engineers on the job. The plan was to build the 500 examples needed to homologate the car for FIA Group 4 (Special GT), so it could serve as a road-legal base for racing and rallying ([Porsche Newsroom, "Fifty years of the 911 Carrera RS 2.7"](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-50-years-911-carrera-rs-2-7-germanys-fastest-sports-car-28486.html); background on the 917 rule change and the GT focus in [stuttcars' RS deep dive](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-carrera-rs-2-7/)).

### Launch and sales
The RS was unveiled at the Paris Motor Show (Porte de Versailles) on **5 October 1972**. All 500 cars had sold by the end of November, and by July 1973 Porsche had tripled production. The final total was **1,580**: 1,308 Touring (option M472), 200 lightweight Sport (M471), 55 RSR racing cars and 17 base vehicles. Once the 1,000th car was built, the RS was homologated for Group 3 as well as Group 4 ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-50-years-911-carrera-rs-2-7-germanys-fastest-sports-car-28486.html); same breakdown in [auto motor und sport](https://www.auto-motor-und-sport.de/oldtimer/porsche-911-carrera-rs-2-7-1972/)). Stuttcars adds that 51 orders were taken at the Paris show, that production ran from November 1972 to July 1973, and that the 1,000th RS was finished on 9 April 1973 ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-carrera-rs-2-7/)). Other published totals differ. German Wikipedia gives 1,590 (1,308 Touring, 217 Sport, 55 RSR 2.8 and 10 prototypes) ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))). Counting road cars only gives 1,525 ([Klassiekerweb](https://www.klassiekerweb.nl/en/merken/porsche/porsche-911-carrera-rs-2-7/)).

### Price and versions
Porsche priced the car at **34,000 DM**. The Sport package (M471) cost 700 DM and the Touring package (M472) 2,500 DM, and the package chosen defined the version ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-50-years-911-carrera-rs-2-7-germanys-fastest-sports-car-28486.html)). The Sport deleted the rear seats, carpets, clock, coat hooks and armrests, offered lightweight seat shells on request and, at first, had a glued-on bonnet crest. It weighed 960 kg, 115 kg less than the Touring ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-50-years-911-carrera-rs-2-7-germanys-fastest-sports-car-28486.html)). Porsche offered the Touring, with 911 S-style equipment, because it expected a fully stripped car to be hard to sell ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963)); [stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-carrera-rs-2-7/)).

### Engineering
Hans Mezger and Valentin Schäffer developed the fuel-injected 2.7-litre flat-six ([Porsche Museum exhibition release](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-museum-special-exhibition-50-years-911-carrera-rs-27-29751.html)). It has a 90 × 70.4 mm bore and stroke, 2,687 cc, Nikasil-coated cylinders and Bosch mechanical injection (engine type 911/83), and makes 210 PS (154 kW) at 6,300 rpm and 255 Nm at 5,100 rpm ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-50-years-911-carrera-rs-2-7-germanys-fastest-sports-car-28486.html); [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic)); [Porsche Museum data](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/Porsche-Museum/Porsche-911-Carrera-RS-2.7.html)). Factory performance was 0–100 km/h in 5.8 s and more than 245 km/h for the Sport, and 6.3 s and 240 km/h for the Touring. That made the RS the first production car to break auto motor und sport's six-second 0–100 mark, and the fastest German production car of its day ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-50-years-911-carrera-rs-2-7-germanys-fastest-sports-car-28486.html)). The magazine's own early-1973 test recorded 5.7 s and weighed the car at 1,010 kg with a full tank ([auto motor und sport](https://www.auto-motor-und-sport.de/oldtimer/porsche-911-carrera-rs-2-7-1972/)).

To save weight Porsche used thinner sheet steel, thin glass, plastic parts and no insulation, bringing the racing versions below the 900 kg homologation target ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-50-years-911-carrera-rs-2-7-germanys-fastest-sports-car-28486.html)). The lighter glass came from the Belgian firm Glaverbel ([auto motor und sport](https://www.auto-motor-und-sport.de/oldtimer/porsche-911-carrera-rs-2-7-1972/)). The chassis got wider rear wheels. It was the first Porsche production car with different tyre sizes front and rear: 185/70 VR 15 on 6J × 15 Fuchs wheels at the front and 215/60 VR 15 on 7J × 15 at the rear, with the rear wings widened by 42 mm to fit them ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-50-years-911-carrera-rs-2-7-germanys-fastest-sports-car-28486.html)).

### The ducktail
Aerodynamics engineers **Hermann Burst** and **Tilman Brodbeck** developed the rear spoiler with stylist **Rolf Wiener** ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-50-years-911-carrera-rs-2-7-germanys-fastest-sports-car-28486.html)). Brodbeck had joined Porsche in October 1970. In the wind tunnel the 911's rear lift coefficient measured 0.29; a rough spoiler made of welding wire and sheet metal cut it by two-thirds to 0.08 after two days of testing. It also lowered drag and raised top speed by 4.5 km/h ([Porsche Newsroom/Christophorus 404](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-rear-ducktail-911-carrera-rs-27-christophorus-404-30154.html)). Brodbeck told IMSA that head of development **Helmuth Bott** set the brief to cut front and rear lift after customer 911s were being out-cornered by BMW 2002s and Ford Capris. Test driver Günter Steckkönig confirmed the gains at Weissach and Hockenheim, and Wiener's production part ended up slightly smaller than the ideal prototype ([IMSA](https://www.imsa.com/news/2022/06/08/the-tale-of-the-porsche-ducktail/)).

The patent disclosure, No. 2238704, was filed on **5 August 1972** ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-50-years-911-carrera-rs-2-7-germanys-fastest-sports-car-28486.html)). Sales staff had predicted they could not sell more than 100 cars with it ([Christophorus 404](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-rear-ducktail-911-carrera-rs-27-christophorus-404-30154.html)). The RS became the first series-production car with front and rear spoilers ([Porsche Museum exhibition release](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-museum-special-exhibition-50-years-911-carrera-rs-27-29751.html)), and Porsche calls the ducktail the first rear spoiler fitted as standard on a production vehicle ([Porsche 60-years press kit, gen 1 engines](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/1.-Generation---Motoren.html)).

### What changed visually vs the 1969–73 911 (stop 03)
The 3D model should change in these ways:
- **Ducktail** spoiler on a glass-fibre engine lid, with "RS" on the spoiler ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963)); [Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-50-years-911-carrera-rs-2-7-germanys-fastest-sports-car-28486.html)).
- **Front spoiler** (chin spoiler) with a central opening for the oil cooler ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).
- **Flared rear arches**, 42 mm wider in total, giving a body width of 1,652 mm versus 1,610 mm for the narrow cars ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-50-years-911-carrera-rs-2-7-germanys-fastest-sports-car-28486.html); [de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).
- **Staggered Fuchs wheels**, 6J front and 7J rear.
- **"Carrera" script** along the flanks between the wheel arches, the first time a 911 carried the name. Harm Lagaaij, then a Porsche designer, recalled choosing that spot ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-50-years-911-carrera-rs-2-7-germanys-fastest-sports-car-28486.html)).
- **Paint**: 29 colours were offered and 27 produced, including Bright Yellow, Red and Blood Orange. The wheel centres often matched the script colour, for example white cars with red, blue or green lettering ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-50-years-911-carrera-rs-2-7-germanys-fastest-sports-car-28486.html)).

### Racing offshoots and successors
The Carrera RSR first raced at the Tour de Corse in November 1972. It won the Daytona 24 Hours in February 1973 (Peter Gregg and Hurley Haywood, 22 laps ahead) and the May 1973 Targa Florio (Herbert Müller and Gijs van Lennep). In its first season it took three international and seven national championships ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-50-years-911-carrera-rs-2-7-germanys-fastest-sports-car-28486.html)). The Porsche Museum's RSR is shown with a 2,992 cc, 228 kW (310 HP) engine ([Porsche Museum](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/Porsche-Museum/Porsche-911-Carrera-RSR.html)).

For 1974 Porsche built the G-series-based **Carrera RS 3.0** (230 PS) ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))). Secret Classics reports 109 cars in total, 55 RS 3.0 road cars plus 54 competition versions, at a base price of 64,980 DM ([Secret Classics](https://www.secret-classics.com/en/porsche-911-carrera-rs-3-0-2/)); stuttcars says 56 ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-carrera-rs-3-0-1974/)).

For buyers who missed the RS, the G-series **911 Carrera 2.7** kept the same 210 PS MFI engine until mid-1975 outside the US ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963)); [en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))).

## 05-g-series-1974 — G-series 911 (1974–1977: 911 / 911 S / Carrera 2.7)

### The first thorough makeover
After ten years of the original 911, Porsche "revised the 911 from the ground up, without watering down the silhouette". Series production of the new car, known internally as the G-Series, began after the summer holidays in **August 1973** for model year 1974 ([Porsche 60-years press kit, gen 2](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/2.-Generation-911,-(G-Serie),-1973---1989.html)). Strictly, "G-Series" names only model year 1974. Porsche's letters then ran H (1975), J (1976), K (1977) and, from 1980, the "A-Programme" and onward, but "G-Series" stuck as the name of the whole second generation ([Porsche Newsroom, "50 years of the Porsche 911 (G-Series)"](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). English Wikipedia labels MY1976 the "I Series", whereas German Wikipedia says the letter I was never used ([en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911); [de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).

No Porsche page fetched states that the car premiered at the September 1973 IAA. Stuttcars' info box claims it did, but the same box contains obvious template errors ([stuttcars G-series hub](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-model-research/porsche-911-research/porsche-911-g-series-2nd-generation-research-hub/)), so treat the IAA premiere as unconfirmed.

### Why the bumpers changed
The most striking change was the **bellows-style bumpers**. US safety rules required new cars to survive a 5 mph (8 km/h) impact front or rear without damage. Porsche's answer was bumpers with plastic bellows on their sides plus a rubber lip on the nose, able to compress by up to 50 mm without harming important components. US cars absorbed the energy with elastic impact absorbers, which were optional in other markets ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). German Wikipedia adds that non-US cars used cheaper deformation tubes, which bent in parking knocks and had to be replaced. It also records the German nickname "Faltenbalg-Porsche" (bellows Porsche) ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).

### Who designed it
Porsche credits its head of design **Anatole "Tony" Lapine**, head of the Style Porsche studio from 1969 to 1988, with designing the bellows bumpers and the impact surfaces of the US version. Under him the 911 lost decorative chrome and gained matt-black window frames and door handles, plus a rear spoiler framed in black rubber ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). Wikipedia says Lapine became Porsche's chief designer on 15 April 1969. The 1974 G-series 911 was one of his first major projects, with the bumpers behind "accordion/bellows covers" and a "trend setting black-on-body-color trim style". Wolfgang Möbius, who came with him from Opel, is credited as principal designer of the 928, not of the 911 G ([en.wikipedia, Anatole Lapine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatole_Lapine)). The basic silhouette remained F. A. Porsche's.

### What changed visually vs the 1973 car (stops 03/04)
- **Bumpers**: body-colour impact bumpers with black bellows and a rubber lip on the nose ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)).
- **Length**: 4,291 mm, 144 mm longer than the forerunner ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)).
- **Front wings and indicators**: the rounded front wings with upright headlights were only slightly modified. The front indicators moved from the wing corners into the bumper ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).
- **Tail lights**: a red light strip with the PORSCHE logo now runs between them ([Porsche 60-years press kit, gen-2 design](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/2.-Generation---Design-und-Modellvarianten.html)).
- **Width**: the base 911 and 911 S kept the narrow rear wings of the 2.4 (1,610 mm wide) ([en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic)); [de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))). The Carrera had rear wings 42 mm wider in total, a front spoiler lip and, where regulations allowed, a rear-lid wing ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)).
- **Spoilers**: the 1974 Carrera could have the RS ducktail. It was standard in North America and optional elsewhere, except in Germany, where the TÜV had outlawed it. That led to the flat "whale tail", optional on the 1974–75 Carrera and fitted to the new Turbo ([en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911)).
- **Base-car wheels**: 5.5 × 15 in steel wheels with chromed hub caps. Options included "Black Look" chrome delete, headlight washers and fog lights; steel wheels were dropped from the range for MY1975 ([Supercar Nostalgia](https://supercarnostalgia.com/blog/porsche-911-g-h-series-1974-1975)).

### Interior and safety
Seats with integrated headrests and automatic three-point belts became standard ([Porsche press kit, gen 2](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/2.-Generation-911,-(G-Serie),-1973---1989.html)). The new sports steering wheel gained a large impact pad and the doors got storage compartments with hinged lids. The Carrera added a three-spoke leather wheel and electric windows ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). A new 80-litre fuel tank was shaped around a space-saver spare ([Supercar Nostalgia](https://supercarnostalgia.com/blog/porsche-911-g-h-series-1974-1975)).

### Engines and prices
From August 1973 every European 911 had a 2.7-litre flat-six, previously exclusive to the Carrera RS 2.7 ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). Outputs, performance and prices (all Porsche figures):

| Model | Power | 0–100 km/h | Top speed | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 911 | 150 PS / 110 kW | 8.5 s | 210 km/h | 26,980 DM |
| 911 S | 175 PS / 129 kW | 7.6 s | 225 km/h | – |
| 911 Carrera | 210 PS | 6.3 s | 240 km/h | – |

The base coupé's 26,980 DM compared with 5,650 DM for a 34 PS VW Beetle ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)).

The 911 and 911 S used Bosch K-Jetronic continuous injection and ran on regular petrol ([Porsche press kit, gen-2 engines](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/2.-Generation-Motoren.html)). K-Jetronic had first appeared on US-market 911 Ts in January 1973 ([en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))). Outside the US, the Carrera kept the RS's mechanically injected 911/83 engine. The US Carrera was restricted to the 175 PS K-Jetronic unit, later 165 PS, and 160 PS in California ([en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))).

The base engine (911/92) gives 150 PS at 5,700 rpm and 235 Nm at 3,800 rpm on an 8.0:1 compression ratio ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963)); [Supercar Nostalgia](https://supercarnostalgia.com/blog/porsche-911-g-h-series-1974-1975)). A four-speed manual was standard and a five-speed optional ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). Supercar Nostalgia gives the codes as Type 915/16 and 915/06, and says four-speed Sportomatic was available on the 911 and 911 S but not the Carrera ([Supercar Nostalgia](https://supercarnostalgia.com/blog/porsche-911-g-h-series-1974-1975)). Porsche switched to a three-speed Sportomatic from August 1975 ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)).

### The oil crisis and year-by-year changes
The launch coincided with the autumn-1973 oil crisis. Porsche's overall orders fell 40% in 1974 and 911 orders 30%, while Germany imposed a 100 km/h autobahn limit and Sunday driving bans ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)).

Model-year changes:
- **MY1975**: steel wheels were dropped and the ducktail option gave way to the RS 3.0-style whale tail. The base 911 left the US market ([Supercar Nostalgia](https://supercarnostalgia.com/blog/porsche-911-g-h-series-1974-1975)).
- **1975 special edition**: the Diamond Silver "25th anniversary" 911/911 S. Specialist sources give 1,063 cars ([Supercar Nostalgia](https://supercarnostalgia.com/blog/porsche-911-g-h-series-1974-1975); [stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-model-research/porsche-911-research/porsche-911-g-series-2nd-generation-research-hub/)); German Wikipedia gives 400 plus 500 US cars ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).
- **Galvanising, 1975**: the 911 became the first series-production car with a body hot-dip galvanised on both sides. Porsche could then offer a six-year corrosion guarantee, extended to seven years for MY1981 and later ten ([Porsche Newsroom, "The innovations of the 911"](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-911-innovations-production-anniversary-13743.html)).
- **August 1975 (MY1976)**: the Carrera got the 3.0-litre engine with 200 PS ([Porsche gen-2 design page](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/2.-Generation---Design-und-Modellvarianten.html)); 3,687 Carrera 3.0s were built ([en.wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))). The base 911 went to 165 PS for MY1976–77 ([de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))). The US got the four-cylinder 912 E from $10,845 ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)).
- **1974–75, the Turbo**: presented at the Paris Motor Show in October 1974 and on the road from March 1975, with 260 PS and 12 cm wider bodywork (covered at stop 06) ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)).
- **August 1977**: the narrow-body 2.7 was sold until then. The 911 SC then adopted the Carrera's widened wings and the Carrera name was dropped (stop 07) ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)).

### Production numbers
Porsche publishes three totals for the whole G-series (1973–1989):
- 198,414 ([Porsche Newsroom, "From zero to 1,000,000"](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-seven-generations-13735.html))
- 198,496 ([Porsche Newsroom, 2023](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html))
- 196,397 ([Porsche 60-years press kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/2.-Generation-911,-(G-Serie),-1973---1989.html))

German Wikipedia, citing Barth/Büsing, gives 193,605 ([de.wikipedia, Porsche 911](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911)). For model year 1974 alone, Supercar Nostalgia lists 911 Coupé 4,014 / Targa 3,110; 911 S 1,359 / 898; Carrera 1,564 / 433 ([Supercar Nostalgia](https://supercarnostalgia.com/blog/porsche-911-g-h-series-1974-1975)). The G-series became the longest-running 911 generation, at almost 16 years ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)).

## 06-930-turbo-1975 — 911 Turbo (type 930), 1975–1989

### Origins: a racing idea turned flagship
Porsche says the idea of a turbocharged road 911 owed much to incoming CEO **Ernst Fuhrmann**, who had seen the turbocharged 917 Spyder win in America; Peter Falk, head of testing at the time, recalls that the car "was developed in an era that actually ought to have heralded the end of the 911" and that early test cars, whose chassis was "somewhat overwhelmed", reached almost 300 km/h ([Porsche, "Turbo – up close and personal"](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/Turbo-%E2%80%93-up-close-and-personal.html)). German Wikipedia (citing Nicky Wright and Barth/Büsing) also credits Fuhrmann with the idea, and explains that a lightweight turbo 911 was first planned for the 1976 Group 4 racing class before FIA weight changes shifted the focus to a road-going flagship that could take on Ferrari and Lamborghini; the many engine changes justified a separate type number, **930** ([DE Wikipedia: Porsche 930](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_930)). English Wikipedia adds that Group 4 required 400 cars in 24 months, that homologation came on 6 December 1975 and that the 1,000th 911 Turbo was completed on 5 May 1976 ([EN Wikipedia: Porsche 911 (930)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(930))). Porsche itself says the car was "initially only planned for a small production run" ([Porsche, "The beginning of the series"](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/The-beginning-of-the-series.html)).

### Key dates
- **13 September 1973, IAA Frankfurt:** a silver **911 RSR Turbo** concept, resembling a Carrera RS 3.0 but with big "Turbo" lettering on the rear wing and Black Watch tartan seats, announces the model ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/The-beginning-of-the-series.html); [Porsche, tartan history](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/A-pattern-with-a-history.html)). Stuttcars dates this show car to "October 1973", which contradicts Porsche ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-turbo-3-0-930/)).
- **29 August 1974:** Ferry Porsche gives his sister **Louise Piëch** the one-off **911 Turbo "No. 1"** for her 70th birthday: narrow Carrera body with "Carrera" script, the new rear wing, and a turbocharged **2.7-litre test engine of 177 kW (240 PS)** ([Porsche, "First Lady"](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2024/history/porsche-louise-piech-911-turbo-no-1-36900.html); [Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/The-beginning-of-the-series.html)).
- **3 October 1974, Paris Motor Show:** the production 911 Turbo with a 3.0-litre engine is unveiled, shown in Viper Green Diamond ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/The-beginning-of-the-series.html); [Porsche Museum data sheet](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:3c07547c-36bc-4e00-825c-5ac2bbe10805/Datenblatt%20911%20Turbo%203.0%20Coup%C3%A9.pdf)).
- **Spring 1975:** global market launch; Porsche's G-series history says it reached the road "as a coupé in March 1975" at **65,800 DM** ([Porsche, 50 years of the G-Series](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). By comparison, the MY1974 150 PS 911 cost 26,980 DM ([same source](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)).

### The car
The Porsche Museum data sheet gives **2,994 cc, 191 kW (260 PS) at 5,500 rpm, 250 km/h, 0–100 km/h in 5.5 s and 1,140 kg**. It calls the car the "fastest road-approved series production car in Germany" at launch and notes that on the first cars (this one was among the first 30) the wider wings were **welded to the body by hand** ([Porsche Museum data sheet](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:3c07547c-36bc-4e00-825c-5ac2bbe10805/Datenblatt%20911%20Turbo%203.0%20Coup%C3%A9.pdf)). Porsche's press kit instead quotes 0–100 km/h in 5.4 s and "over 250 km/h" ([Porsche, "The 911 Turbo generations"](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/The-911-Turbo-generations.html)). The engine used a single KKK turbocharger with a 0.8-bar wastegate/bypass limit, a 6.5:1 compression ratio and Bosch K-Jetronic injection, driving through a strengthened **four-speed** gearbox. Porsche said four gears were enough because of the engine's broad torque ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_930); [stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-turbo-3-0-930/)). The chassis took rigid wheel hubs from the 917 and axle kinematics from the Carrera RSR ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/The-911-Turbo-generations.html)).

### What it looked like (vs. the 1974 G-series 911)
- **Width:** flared wings made the car **12 cm wider** ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-g/911-turbo/); [Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). It measured 4,291 × 1,775 × 1,320 mm on a 2,272 mm wheelbase, with tracks of 1,432/1,501 mm ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_930); [Excellence spec sheet](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/371)). The narrow 1974 911 was 1,610 mm wide ([DE Wikipedia: Porsche 911 (1963)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).
- **Rear wing:** a fixed "whale tail" with a black PU/hard-rubber border sat on a glass-fibre-reinforced engine lid ([Porsche Museum data sheet](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:3c07547c-36bc-4e00-825c-5ac2bbe10805/Datenblatt%20911%20Turbo%203.0%20Coup%C3%A9.pdf); [Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-g/911-turbo/)). Aerodynamicist **Hermann Burst**'s team, which had invented the Carrera RS 2.7 ducktail, developed this "whale fin" and matched it to the front spoiler for high-speed stability ([Porsche, Hermann Burst](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/Contemporary-witness-Hermann-Burst.html)).
- **Details:** bellows bumpers, body-colour mirrors and headlamp rings, black-anodised window surrounds and door handles, a rear light strip with a black Porsche logo, and from MY1977 stone-chip foils ahead of the rear wheels ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-g/911-turbo/)).
- **Wheels and tyres:** forged Fuchs rims, 7J×15 front and 8J×15 rear. The MY1975 cars came on 185/70 VR 15 and 215/60 VR 15 tyres, with 205/50 and 225/50 VR 15 optional. Pirelli P7s in those 50-series sizes became standard for MY1976, and 16-inch wheels for MY1977 ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_930)).
- **Interior:** leather, automatic heating control, heated front and rear windows and the Carrera sports steering wheel were standard ([Porsche Museum data sheet](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:3c07547c-36bc-4e00-825c-5ac2bbe10805/Datenblatt%20911%20Turbo%203.0%20Coup%C3%A9.pdf)). Tartan seat fabrics were Turbo-exclusive options before reaching other 911s for MY1976. The Style Porsche team under chief designer **Anatole Lapine** adopted them after Lapine turned up in tartan trousers, and stylist **Dorothea Müller-Goodwyn** found a supplier in Dettingen unter Teck ([Porsche, "A pattern with history"](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/A-pattern-with-a-history.html)). Lapine led Style Porsche from 1969 to 1988 ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)).

### Evolution 1976–1989
- **MY1976:** galvanised body and six-year anti-rust warranty; exports to the US and Canada begin at 180 kW (245 PS), with 520 cars going to the US in 1976 ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_930)). Early US cars wore "Turbo Carrera" badges ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(930))). Car and Driver rated the US car at 234 hp ([Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/a15366914/flat-sixy-the-evolution-of-porsche-911-engine-size-technology-and-output-in-the-u-s/)).
- **MY1977:** boost gauge, 16-inch wheels and power brakes ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/The-911-Turbo-generations.html)). The first three model years produced **2,850** Turbo 3.0s ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/The-911-Turbo-generations.html); [Porsche 2014](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-celebrates-40-years-of-the-911-turbo-10880.html)).
- **MY1978, Turbo 3.3:** capacity rose to 3,299 cc and a charge-air intercooler was placed under the rear wing. Porsche calls this a world first in a production car. Output was **221 kW (300 PS) at 5,500 rpm and 412 Nm**, with 917-derived brakes and a 260 km/h top speed ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/The-911-Turbo-generations.html)). The engine lid was now sheet steel and the larger fixed plastic wing (the "tea tray") made room for the intercooler ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-g/911-turbo/); [EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(930))). Peter Falk recalls winning Fuhrmann's approval for the 917 brakes "after three whiskys" at the 928 launch in spring 1977 ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/Turbo-%E2%80%93-up-close-and-personal.html)).
- **c. 1980–1985:** emissions rules kept the Turbo out of the US; it returned for 1986 with 282 hp ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(930)); [Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/a15366914/flat-sixy-the-evolution-of-porsche-911-engine-size-technology-and-output-in-the-u-s/)).
- **From 1983:** the Sonderwunsch (special-request) programme offered the **Slantnose/"Flachbau"**, a flat 935-style front with pop-up headlights from the 944. An extra **30 PS (330 PS)** option gave a potential 275 km/h ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/The-911-Turbo-generations.html); [Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). German Wikipedia counts 58 + 204 + 686 = 948 Flachbau cars, and English Wikipedia also gives 948 ([DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_930); [EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(930))).
- **February/spring 1987:** the **Turbo Targa and Turbo Cabriolet** arrive, the Cabriolet with an electric roof as a no-cost option ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html); [Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/The-911-Turbo-generations.html)).
- **MY1989:** a five-speed gearbox replaces the four-speed, for this final year only ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-g/911-turbo/); [EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(930))). Porsche says **17,792** Turbo 3.3s were built over its "13 epic years" ([porsche.com](https://www.porsche.com/stories/innovation/brief-history-of-porsche-911-turbo/)). German Wikipedia gives 17,381 and English Wikipedia 18,770.
- The successor 964 Turbo (1990/91) kept a 3.3-litre engine at 320 PS ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/The-911-Turbo-generations.html)).

### Reputation
The early car's sudden boost and short-wheelbase rear-engine layout earned it the nickname "Widowmaker" ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(930)); [stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-turbo-3-0-930/)). Porsche's own phrase was a "sports car for 'real men'", adding wryly that many of its buyers were women, starting with Louise Piëch ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/The-911-Turbo-generations.html)). Car and Driver timed the 1975 and 1978 US cars at 4.9 s to 60 mph, tied as the quickest cars it tested in the 1970s ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(930))). Its 1978 test measured a 165 mph top speed ([Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/1978-porsche-930-turbo-tested-today-review/)).

## 07-911-sc-1978 — 911 SC, 1977/78–1983

### Context: the 911 was supposed to die
By the late 1970s Porsche's future was meant to be front-engined and water-cooled. Porsche's own G-series history says the transaxle 924/944 and V8 928 "were set to succeed the 911" but "could not convincingly replace the charisma of the 911" ([Porsche, 50 years of the G-Series](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). According to German Wikipedia (citing Kubiak), management planned to let the 911 run out in 1981 and keep only the 924, 944 and 928, until Ernst Fuhrmann was replaced by **Peter W. Schutz** at the end of 1980 ([DE Wikipedia: Porsche 911](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911)). Porsche's obituary for Schutz confirms that he took over as CEO in January 1981 and "in just his third week as Porsche CEO … reversed the company's earlier decision to cease production of the 911" ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/company/porsche-peter-schutz-ceo-obituary-14435.html)). Schutz's own account, quoted by English Wikipedia from Tobias Corlett's book, is famous. On a chart in engineering chief **Helmuth Bott**'s office, the 911 programme stopped at the end of 1981, so Schutz took a marker pen and extended the 911 bar "clean off the chart" ([EN Wikipedia: Porsche 911 (classic)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))). Car and Driver's 1978 road test likewise called the SC possibly "the last new model in the 911 series" ([Car and Driver, 1978 911 SC](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/a15141220/1978-porsche-911-sc-archived-road-test-review/)).

### Launch and concept
For **MY1978** the range was cut to two models. The **911 SC** ("Super Carrera") replaced both the 911 and the Carrera, leaving the 911 Turbo above it ([Porsche Classic: 911 SC](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-g/911-sc/); [Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). Porsche says the 2.7-litre narrow-body 911 was sold "until August 1977" and that the SC arrived in 1977. The same Porsche text also contains a sentence dating the renaming to "July 1976", which is most likely an error ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). Stuttcars dates the start of MY1978 SC production to August 1977 ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-sc-g-series-1978-1983/)). Porsche's 2022 article on its naming conventions confirms the SC was "introduced in 1977". It draws a parallel with the 95 PS **356 SC** of MY1964, which was meant to end the 356 line: the 911 SC "was also intended initially to be the last 911 model", until "the series was continued with the 911 Carrera 3.2" ([Porsche, code terminology](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/products/porsche-code-terminology-model-name-in-house-type-number-27464.html)). *(The directive's suggested "IAA 1977 debut" could not be verified from any fetched source.)*

### Engine and chassis
The SC used a **3.0-litre (2,994 cc, 95 × 70.4 mm)** air-cooled flat-six with Bosch K-Jetronic: **132 kW (180 PS) at 5,500 rpm and 265 Nm at 4,200 rpm**, compression 8.5:1 ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html); [DE Wikipedia: Porsche 911 (1963)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963)); [EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))). The crankcase was now aluminium instead of magnesium ([Car and Driver, 50 years of the 911](https://www.caranddriver.com/features/a15112123/golden-anniversary-50-years-of-the-porsche-911-feature/); [Car and Driver, 911 engine evolution](https://www.caranddriver.com/news/a15366914/flat-sixy-the-evolution-of-porsche-911-engine-size-technology-and-output-in-the-u-s/)). Stuttcars notes it was 20 PS down on the preceding European Carrera 3.0 but had more torque at low revs, and that a brake servo was fitted for the first time ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-sc-g-series-1978-1983/)). Output rose to **188 PS for MY1980** and **204 PS (150 kW) at 5,900 rpm for MY1981–83**, when compression went to 9.8:1 and the engine switched to premium fuel ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-g/911-sc/); [Porsche 60-years kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/2.-Generation---Design-und-Modellvarianten.html); [DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911)). US cars kept lower-compression engines throughout ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))). Car and Driver's 1978 US test car made **172 hp**, weighed 2,630 lb, reached 60 mph in **5.5 s** (the "fastest normally-aspirated Porsche, 0–60, that we have ever driven") and cost **$19,475** as tested ([Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/a15141220/1978-porsche-911-sc-archived-road-test-review/)). European figures for the 180 PS coupé are 1,160 kg, 225 km/h and 0–100 km/h in 7.0 s ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963)); [Excellence](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/44)). The Sportomatic was dropped during SC production, around MY1980, though sources disagree on the exact year ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911); [stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-sc-g-series-1978-1983/); [Excellence](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/44)).

### What it looked like (vs. stop 05's 1974 911)
- **Wider rear wings for everyone:** Porsche says that "with the introduction of the 911 SC in 1977, Porsche widened the body at the rear for all models" by adopting the Carrera's flared wings ([Porsche, bodystyles](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/2.-Generation---Karosserievarianten.html); [Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). Width is **1,652 mm** against 1,610 mm for the narrow 911/911 S. Length stays 4,291 mm, height 1,320 mm and wheelbase 2,272 mm ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).
- **Trim:** MY1978 cars still had chrome window surrounds, door handles and headlamp rings. From **MY1979** these became black anodised, with headlamp rings in body colour. Mirrors were body-colour and electrically adjustable ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-g/911-sc/)).
- **Wheels:** 15-inch **ATS cast-aluminium** wheels (6J front / 7J rear) on 185/70 and 215/60 VR 15 tyres; 16-inch Fuchs with Pirelli P7 was an option ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-g/911-sc/); [Excellence](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/44); [stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-sc-g-series-1978-1983/)).
- **MY1981+:** side indicator repeaters on the front wings, and the 911 Turbo 3.0's front and rear spoilers as options ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-g/911-sc/)). Car and Driver noted that without the options there was "no humungus whale-tail", just wider rear tyres and flared fenders ([Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/a15141220/1978-porsche-911-sc-archived-road-test-review/)).
- The bellows bumpers and red rear reflector strip of the G-series remained ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-g/911-sc/)).

### Cabriolet: the first fully open 911
Porsche showed a **911 Cabriolet concept in September 1981** (IAA Frankfurt). It had a turbo engine and all-wheel drive as a signal of the new strategy ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html); [DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911)). German Wikipedia says the car was presented at the Geneva Motor Show in March 1982 ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))). Customer deliveries of the **911 SC Cabriolet** began in **January 1983**, "the first time in 18 years that the Porsche line-up had featured a convertible" ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). Porsche Classic calls it "the first completely open-top 911 to have a fabric roof", alongside the Targa ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-g/911-sc/)). Porsche credits Schutz with introducing the 911 Cabriolet to the US market ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/company/porsche-peter-schutz-ceo-obituary-14435.html)). First-year (MY1983) volume was 4,277 per DE Wikipedia (1,877 of them to the US), 4,214 per EN Wikipedia and 4,187 per stuttcars.

### Special versions and motorsport
- **Weissach Edition (MY1980, M439):** North America only, **407** cars in Platinum or Black Metallic ([Porsche Klassik via Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-klassik-911-sc-weissach-edition-merlin-assfalg-28981.html)); EN Wikipedia says 408.
- **"50 Jahre Porsche" edition (1981/82):** 200 911 SCs in Meteor metallic with "F. Porsche" embroidered headrests, quoted from Porsche press information 21/81 ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).
- **Rallying:** two works 911 SCs finished 2nd and 4th on the 1978 East African Safari. The 1984 **911 SC/RS (type 954)** was a Group B rally evolution with 250 PS ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html); [DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).

### Production
SC sales totalled **58,914** according to Paternie's *Porsche 911 Red Book*, as cited by English Wikipedia ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))). Stuttcars' model-year table sums to 60,265 ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-sc-g-series-1978-1983/)). Stuttcars also says the SC outsold the 928 "by nearly fifty percent". In **September 1983** the 911 Carrera 3.2 replaced it ([Porsche 60-years kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/2.-Generation---Design-und-Modellvarianten.html)).

### Design lead
Style Porsche was headed by **Anatole Lapine** from 1969 to 1988. Under him the 911 lost decorative chrome in favour of matt-black window frames and door handles, which is visible in the SC's change from chrome to black trim in MY1979 ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html); [Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-g/911-sc/)).

## 08-carrera-32-1984 — 911 Carrera 3.2, 1983/84–1989

### A reprieve becomes a revival
With the 911's future secured by Peter Schutz's 1981 decision ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/company/porsche-peter-schutz-ceo-obituary-14435.html)), **"in September 1983, the 911 Carrera 3.2 replaced the 911 SC"** ([Porsche 60-years kit, 2nd generation](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/2.-Generation---Design-und-Modellvarianten.html)). For MY1984 Porsche "reverted to calling the 911 SC the 911 Carrera", the first use of the Carrera name since 1977 ([Porsche, 50 years of the G-Series](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html); [EN Wikipedia: Porsche 911 (classic)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))). German Wikipedia ties the change to tighter emissions rules in the US, Japan and Europe. Porsche raised displacement and switched from K-Jetronic to Bosch Motronic for the E-programme starting in mid-1983 ([DE Wikipedia: Porsche 911 (1963)](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))). *(The directive's "IAA 1983 debut" could not be confirmed from a fetched source. Porsche only says the car arrived "after the 1983 summer break".)* ([Porsche, engines](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/2.-Generation-Motoren.html))

### Engine: 80 per cent new
The 3,164 cc flat-six combined the SC's 95 mm bore with the Turbo 3.3's 74.4 mm crankshaft stroke. Compression rose to 10.3:1 (9.5:1 for the US), and Porsche claimed the engine was 80% new ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic)); [Car and Driver, Feb 1984](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/a15142185/1984-porsche-911-carrera-archived-road-test-review/)). It made **170 kW (231 PS) at 5,900 rpm and 284 Nm at 4,800 rpm** without a catalytic converter ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963)); [Porsche Museum data sheet](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:361ab253-d675-4d19-8be8-79a271cf15e8/Datenblatt%20Porsche%20911%20Carrera%20Cabriolet%20Turbolook.pdf)). US models with a catalytic converter had **207 PS** ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)), later 217 PS ([DE Wikipedia: Porsche 911](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911)). Porsche lists "digital engine electronics" as the Carrera 3.2's innovation. The Motronic system with L-Jetronic injection and an air-flow sensor gave cleaner running and overrun fuel cut-off, and Porsche adds that with proper care "cars doing well over 300,000 kilometres without an engine overhaul were the rule rather than the exception" ([Porsche, innovations](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/2.-Generation---Innovationen.html); [Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). Hydraulic chain tensioners cured an old 911 weakness ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).

**Gearbox:** the type-915 five-speed carried over for MY1984–86. From MY1987 (summer 1986) it gave way to the **G50** with a hydraulic clutch, and reverse moved to front-left ([Berlin Motor Books](https://berlinmotorbooks.de/en/porsche-911-carrera-3-2-buying-advice-price-development/); [Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html); [EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))).

**Performance:** the factory quoted 245 km/h ([Porsche Museum data sheet](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:361ab253-d675-4d19-8be8-79a271cf15e8/Datenblatt%20Porsche%20911%20Carrera%20Cabriolet%20Turbolook.pdf); [DE Wikipedia: Porsche 911](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911)) and 0–100 km/h in 6.1 s ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))). With the optional spoilers, *auto motor und sport* measured 254 km/h ([DE Wikipedia: Porsche 911](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911)). Car and Driver's US car (200 hp SAE, 2,760 lb, $37,075 as tested) ran 0–60 mph in 5.3 s and reached 149 mph ([Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/a15142185/1984-porsche-911-carrera-archived-road-test-review/)). The magazine later called it the fastest production car it tested in 1984, bar a grey-market Countach ([Car and Driver, 50 years of the 911](https://www.caranddriver.com/features/a15112123/golden-anniversary-50-years-of-the-porsche-911-feature/)). The US base price at launch was **$31,950** for the Coupé, $33,450 for the Targa and $36,450 for the Cabriolet ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))).

### What changed visually (vs. the SC)
Very little. English Wikipedia and Car and Driver both note that the main external clue is the **fog lamps integrated into the front valance/spoiler** ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic)); [Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/a15142185/1984-porsche-911-carrera-archived-road-test-review/)). Porsche Classic lists the details ([Porsche Classic: 911 Carrera](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-g/911-carrera/)):
- side indicator repeaters on the front wings;
- forged 15-inch **Fuchs** wheels as standard (the SC had ATS cast wheels), with **16-inch** wheels standard from MY1989;
- an optional PU front spoiler and a flat rear wing with a black hard-rubber edge developed for the Carrera;
- from **MY1987**, a rear light strip with a red reflective Porsche logo and built-in rear fog lights.

Dimensions stayed at 4,291 × 1,652 × 1,320 mm on a 2,272 mm wheelbase ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963)); [Excellence](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/385)). Inside, a redesigned dash with larger vents arrived in 1986 ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))). The design department was still led by **Anatole Lapine**, head of Style Porsche from 1969 to 1988 ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)).

### Variants and milestones
- **Turbo-look (M491, "Supersport"):** the wide body, spoilers, suspension and brakes of the Turbo without its engine. Porsche lists the Coupé for 1983–1989, the Targa and Cabriolet for 1984–1989 and the Speedster for 1988–1989. Before the autumn-1983 launch, engineers built a Silver Rose Metallic Carrera Cabriolet Turbo-look as a pre-production car for CEO Peter Schutz ([Porsche, "The name Turbo"](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/The-name-Turbo-in-the-Porsche-family.html); [Porsche Museum data sheet](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:361ab253-d675-4d19-8be8-79a271cf15e8/Datenblatt%20Porsche%20911%20Carrera%20Cabriolet%20Turbolook.pdf)).
- **Carrera Club Sport (1987):** 340 lighter cars without radio, sunroof, A/C or rear seats ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))).
- **"250,000 911s" edition (summer 1987):** marked the 250,000th 911 built. **875** cars (505 Coupés, 165 Targas, 205 Cabriolets) ([Porsche Klassik via Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2024/history/porsche-klassik-911-g-model-anniversary-model-1987-35245.html)) in Diamond Blue Metallic ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).
- **"25 Jahre 911" edition (MY1989)** ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(1963))).
- **Speedster:** shown as a concept at the **IAA in September 1987** and built "from the beginning of 1989 until the end of G-Series production in July 1989" ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). It had a shortened windscreen and a plastic hump cover, was mostly Turbo-look, and prices started at 110,000 marks. Porsche's 2019 Speedster history says 2,103 were built, 161 of them with the narrow body ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2019/history/porsche-speedster-history-open-top-driving-pleasure-17559.html)). Porsche's 2018 and 2023 texts give 171 narrow-body export cars ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-2-g-model-16459.html)). English Wikipedia says the Speedster began under Helmuth Bott in 1983, was championed by Schutz, and that Lapine reworked the prototype ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))).
- **Motorsport:** the 911 Carrera 3.2 4x4 (type 953) won the 1984 Paris–Dakar ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html); [Porsche, 953](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2021/history/porsche-911-carrera-3-2-4x4-paris-dakar-953-walter-roehrl-lake-zell-cold-start-by-gp-23758.html)).

### End of the G-series
G-series production ended in **July 1989**. Its end also closed body production at the former Reutter plant ([Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). Carrera 3.2 output totalled **76,473** per Corlett, cited by English Wikipedia ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic))). Stuttcars says "more than 76,000", but its model-year table sums to 80,383 including 2,104 Speedsters ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-carrera-3-2-1984-1989/)). Porsche's generation total for the G-series is **198,496** ([Porsche infographic](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:17040538-1566-4a2c-8c16-91db63a8ddc4/02_911er_Generationen_G-Serie_EN.pdf); [Porsche](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). Other Porsche texts give 198,414 ([Porsche 2017](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-seven-generations-13735.html)) and 196,397 ([Porsche 60-years kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/2.-Generation-911,-(G-Serie),-1973---1989.html)).

## 09-964-1989 — The 964 (1988–1994): a new 911 under a familiar skin

**Why it was needed.** By the late 1980s the G-model had been in production for more than 15 years. Porsche was in a serious crisis because the 944, its highest-revenue model, was no longer selling in the hoped-for numbers. Customers also increasingly wanted comfort as well as outright performance ([DE Wikipedia: Porsche 964](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_964)). Porsche's 2018 history series calls the third generation "a bet on the future of the company, which was experiencing economic challenges – a bet that this third generation would win" ([Porsche Newsroom, 2018](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-3-type-964-16466.html)). The project had been named internally in March–April 1984: 964 for the next 911 Carrera and 965 for a Turbo successor ([Stuttcars, 964 story](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-964-the-story/)).

**Launch sequence.** Porsche says the all-wheel-drive Carrera 4 launched in August 1988 at exactly 114,500 DM. The rear-wheel-drive Carrera 2 followed a year later at 103,500 DM ([Porsche 60-Years press kit, gen 3](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/3.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(964),-1988---1994.html)). The public debut was the Paris Motor Show on 28 September 1988, and the car was sold from model year 1989 ([PCA model guide](https://pca.org/news/model-guide-the-964-was-a-new-911-with-classic-looks)). Porsche had released photos in July 1988, and journalists first drove it that November ([Stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-964-the-story/)). Porsche Classic describes the Carrera 4 as the 911's all-wheel-drive debut, unveiled for the car's 25th anniversary, and dates the Carrera 2 (and Tiptronic) to MY1990 and the Turbo to MY1991 ([Porsche Classic 964 overview](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/964/), [Porsche Classic 964 detail](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/964/964/)). For a while the old G-model Carrera 3.2 and the new Carrera 4 were built side by side ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_964)).

**What was new: roughly 85% of the car.** Porsche says almost 85% of the parts were newly developed ([Porsche 60-Years, gen 3](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/3.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(964),-1988---1994.html); [Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/964/964/)). Other sources give 87% ([DE Wikipedia 964](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_964)) or 80% ([DE Wikipedia 911](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911)). The torsion bars used since the 356 and the original 911 gave way to MacPherson struts and coil springs at the front and coil springs on semi-trailing arms at the rear. ABS and power steering became standard ([Porsche 60-Years](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/3.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(964),-1988---1994.html); [DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_964)). The new 3.6-litre M64 flat-six added twin-spark ignition, knock control and a three-way catalytic converter. It made 250 PS (184 kW) at 6,100 rpm and 310 Nm at 4,800 rpm ([Porsche 2018](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-3-type-964-16466.html); [DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_964)), up from 231 bhp in the Carrera 3.2 ([Motor Sport, April 1990](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/april-1990/41/new-front/)). The Carrera 4's all-wheel drive was derived from the 959, with electronically controlled, hydraulically regulated torque distribution ([Porsche 2018](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-3-type-964-16466.html)); the normal split was 31% front and 69% rear ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/964/964/)). From January 1990 the Carrera 2 could be ordered with the four-speed Tiptronic. It was the first automatic in a 911 since the Sportomatic ended in 1980 ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_964)), and Porsche calls it "a first appearance for this style of gearbox in a Porsche" ([Porsche 60-Years, engines](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/3.-Generation---Motoren.html)). Driver and passenger airbags became standard on all 911s from 1991 ([Porsche 60-Years, innovations](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/3.-Generation---Karosserievarianten-und-Innovationen.html)).

**What changed visually (for the 3D morph).** The silhouette, doors and roof carried over, but the ends were new:
- deformable plastic front and rear panels, with the number plate, air intakes, indicators and fog lights integrated into the front bumper for the first time ([Porsche 60-Years, design](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/3.-Generation---Design-und-Modellvarianten.html); [Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/964/964/));
- the headlamps still stood "almost vertically in the tunnels of the wings" ([Porsche 60-Years, design](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/3.-Generation---Design-und-Modellvarianten.html));
- new sill covers, optimised rain gutters and a completely smooth underbody ([Porsche 60-Years, design](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/3.-Generation---Design-und-Modellvarianten.html));
- a new rear light strip with a red reflective PORSCHE logo and red rear indicators ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/964/964/));
- a patented rear spoiler, flush with the engine lid, that rises above about 80 km/h (50 mph) and retracts below about 10 km/h ([Porsche 60-Years](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/3.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(964),-1988---1994.html); [Stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-964-the-story/)).

Drag fell to Cd 0.32 from the 0.395 that Motor Sport quotes for the Carrera 3.2 ([Motor Sport 1990](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/april-1990/41/new-front/)). The five-spoke Fuchs wheels were replaced by 16-inch "Design 90" wheels ([PCA](https://pca.org/news/model-guide-the-964-was-a-new-911-with-classic-looks)); MY1989 Carrera 4s had similar-looking forged "ClubSport" wheels ([Stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-964-the-story/)). Mirrors switched to the "Cup" design from MY1992 ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/964/964/)). Key dimensions for the Carrera 2 Coupé: 4,250 mm long, 1,652 mm wide, about 1,310 mm high, 2,272 mm wheelbase ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_964); [Excellence spec sheet](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/291)).

**Who designed it.** Benjamin Dimson, a Filipino ArtCenter graduate who joined Style Porsche in autumn 1981, says he was "tasked to head up the design of the 964" as exterior design lead ([AutoIndustriya interview](https://www.autoindustriya.com/features/benjamin-dimson-how-a-filipino-artist-built-a-career-designing-porsches-and-mercs.html)). Stuttcars credits him with the bumpers, side sills, rear lamp panel and rear spoiler ([Stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-964-the-story/)), and EN Wikipedia says the design work ran through January 1986 ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(964))). The head of design was Anatole "Tony" Lapine, Porsche's chief designer from 15 April 1969. The 964 was the last project he oversaw before retiring in 1989, when Harm Lagaay succeeded him ([EN Wikipedia: Anatole Lapine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatole_Lapine); [Porsche Newsroom: One icon, four designers](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/one-icon-four-designers-10714.html)).

**Performance and price of the reference car.** A manual Carrera 2 Coupé did 0–100 km/h in 5.7 s and reached 260 km/h; with Tiptronic the figures were 6.6 s and 256 km/h. It weighed 1,350 kg DIN, against 1,450 kg for the Carrera 4 ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_964)). Motor Sport measured 0–60 mph in "just over 5 seconds" and weighed its test car at 1,350 kg unladen; a UK coupé cost £41,504.94 ([Motor Sport 1990](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/april-1990/41/new-front/)).

**Variants and milestones.**
- 1990: the Carrera Cup racer, the start of the one-make series with 265 PS ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_964)).
- March 1990 (Geneva): the 911 Turbo with the 930's 3.3-litre engine uprated to 320 PS, a wider body and a fixed high wing ([Porsche Classic 964 Turbo](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/964/964-turbo/); [EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(964))).
- 1991/92: the lightweight Carrera RS (260 PS, about 1,220 kg) ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_964)).
- 1992: the Turbo S with 381 PS ([Porsche 60-Years, engines](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/3.-Generation---Motoren.html)).
- 1993: the Turbo 3.6 (360 PS), Carrera RS 3.8 (300 PS), RS America (701 built), the "30 Jahre 911" anniversary Carrera 4 (911 units, Turbo body without the big wing, mostly Viola Metallic), and the Speedster (930 built plus about 15 turbo-look cars) ([Porsche 2018](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-3-type-964-16466.html); [DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_964)).

Its successor, the 993, was already "knocking at the door from October" 1993 ([Porsche 2018](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-3-type-964-16466.html)).

**Production.** Porsche counts 63,762 964s built from 1988 to 1994 ([Porsche 60-Years](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/3.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(964),-1988---1994.html); [Porsche infographic](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:56be9a9d-7679-4515-bcba-d3bb37571d88/03_911er_Generationen_964_EN.jpg)). Kittler's model-by-model count, quoted by EN Wikipedia, totals 62,172, including 18,219 Carrera 2 Coupés and 13,353 Carrera 4 Coupés ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(964))).

**Quirks.** DE Wikipedia quotes a memorable ad line by Jung von Matt: "You can have a longer breakfast. You'll be back earlier for dinner. Is there a better family car?" ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_964)). One of Porsche's own pages dates the RS revival to 1989, which contradicts the RS's 1991 price listing and appears to be an error ([Porsche 60-Years, engines](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/3.-Generation---Motoren.html); [DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_964)).

## 10-993-1994 — The 993 (1993–1998): the last air-cooled 911

**Context and investment.** Porsche says 1993 "was not a good year for Porsche. Overall, the brand was in crisis." With the front-engined transaxle cars (924/944/968) being phased out, the 911 at times had to carry the company on its own ([Porsche Newsroom, 30 years of the 993](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/30-Jahre-911-Carrera-der-Generation-993-%E2%80%93-Der-letzte-seiner-Art.html); [Porsche 60-Years, gen 4](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/4.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(993),-1993-1998.html)). Porsche invested around 400 million DM and four years of development. Its then head of development insisted that "The Porsche 911 must remain state-of-the-art" ([Porsche, 30 years of the 993](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/30-Jahre-911-Carrera-der-Generation-993-%E2%80%93-Der-letzte-seiner-Art.html)).

**Debut and roll-out.**
- The fourth generation was unveiled at the IAA in Frankfurt in autumn 1993 ([Porsche 60-Years, gen 4](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/4.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(993),-1993-1998.html)). The press and trade day was 9 September 1993, almost exactly 30 years after the 901's IAA debut. A silver Carrera Coupé stood next to a Supercup racer ([Stuttcars, 993 story](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-993-the-story/)).
- Porsche Classic dates the switch from 964 to 993 to model year 1994 ([Porsche Classic 993](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/993/993/)). Porsche says production began in 1993; EN Wikipedia, citing the *Porsche 911 Red Book*, gives January 1994 – March 1998 ([Porsche, 30 years of the 993](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/30-Jahre-911-Carrera-der-Generation-993-%E2%80%93-Der-letzte-seiner-Art.html); [EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(993))).
- The Coupé came first (1993), then the Cabriolet in early 1994 and the Carrera 4 (MY1995, shown at Paris in October 1994). The 911 Turbo arrived in 1995. The glass-roof Targa and the wide-body Carrera 4S were shown at the IAA in September 1995 as MY1996 cars, and the rear-drive Carrera S followed in 1997 ([Porsche, 30 years of the 993](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/30-Jahre-911-Carrera-der-Generation-993-%E2%80%93-Der-letzte-seiner-Art.html); [Stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-993-the-story/); [Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/993/993/)).
- The launch price of a MY1994 911 Carrera Coupé was 125,760 DM ([Porsche 60-Years, gen 4](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/4.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(993),-1993-1998.html)).

**Design: everything but the roof.** Porsche writes that "The roof line was practically the only thing to remain unchanged" ([Porsche 60-Years, gen 4](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/4.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(993),-1993-1998.html)). The changes, for the 3D morph:
- **Front:** the wings became lower, wider and flatter, and new poly-ellipsoid headlights lay back in them, replacing the upright "tunnel" lamps. Side lights, fog lights and indicators were integrated into the front ([Porsche, 30 years of the 993](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/30-Jahre-911-Carrera-der-Generation-993-%E2%80%93-Der-letzte-seiner-Art.html)). A shorter front lid removed the black rubber lip that had joined body and bumper on the G-model and 964 ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/993/993/)).
- **Body sides and rear:** the surfaces play concave against convex with integrated bumpers and flush-mounted windows, and the wide rear end carries an angled tail-light panel ([Porsche Newsroom, 2018](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-4-type-993-16486.html)). The rear wings are wider and run straighter to the tail, where a diagonal light strip carries integrated tail lights and yellow/orange indicators ([Porsche 60-Years, design](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/4.-Generation---Design-und-Modellvarianten.html); [Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/993/993/)).
- **Details:** an enlarged retracting rear spoiler and teardrop-shaped mirrors ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(993))), plus two oval tailpipes that became slightly rectangular from MY1996 ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/993/993/)).
- **Dimensions:** 4,245 mm long, 1,735 mm wide, about 1,310 mm high on the unchanged 2,272 mm wheelbase. The rear track grew from 1,374 mm (964) to 1,445 mm ([DE Wikipedia: Porsche 993](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_993); [DE Wikipedia: Porsche 964](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_964); [Excellence spec sheet](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/312)).

DE Wikipedia notes that chief designer Harm Lagaay's team looked back to the original 911, where body and bumpers formed one unit ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_993)).

**Who designed it.** Porsche lists the 993 and 993 Turbo among the cars British designer Anthony "Tony" Hatter was responsible for. Hatter moved from Opel to Porsche in 1986 ([Porsche Newsroom: Tony Hatter](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/Contemporary-witness---Tony-Hatter.html)). He worked under Harm Lagaay, Porsche's chief designer from 1989 until he retired in 2004 ([Stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-993-the-story/); [Porsche Newsroom: One icon, four designers](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/one-icon-four-designers-10714.html)). The Dutch spelling of his surname is Lagaaij; Porsche writes Lagaay. A PCNA film also credits Stephen Murkett as 993 Turbo co-designer ([Stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-993-the-story/)).

**Engineering.**
- **Rear axle:** the new aluminium LSA axle ("light weight, stability, agility") is a multi-link design that Porsche calls the ultimate stage of the "Weissach" axle, with passive rear-steer properties ([Porsche, 30 years of the 993](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/30-Jahre-911-Carrera-der-Generation-993-%E2%80%93-Der-letzte-seiner-Art.html)). EN Wikipedia traces it to the unbuilt 989 four-door ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(993))).
- **Engine:** the 3.6-litre flat-six gained a stiffer crankshaft, lighter rods and pistons and hydraulic valve-clearance compensation. It produced 200 kW (272 PS) at 6,100 rpm, against 250 PS for the 964 ([Porsche, 30 years of the 993](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/30-Jahre-911-Carrera-der-Generation-993-%E2%80%93-Der-letzte-seiner-Art.html)), and 330 Nm at 5,000 rpm ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_993)). A dual-flow exhaust carried two catalytic converters ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(993))).
- **Gearbox:** a six-speed manual was standard for the first time in a 911 ([Porsche 60-Years, engines](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/4.-Generation---Motoren.html)). The Tiptronic gained steering-wheel rocker switches as Tiptronic S from MY1995 ([Porsche, 30 years of the 993](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/30-Jahre-911-Carrera-der-Generation-993-%E2%80%93-Der-letzte-seiner-Art.html)).
- **Performance and practicality:** 0–100 km/h in 5.6 s and 270 km/h top speed. Luggage volume rose 20% to 123 litres ([Porsche, 30 years of the 993](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/30-Jahre-911-Carrera-der-Generation-993-%E2%80%93-Der-letzte-seiner-Art.html)).
- **VarioRam (MY1996):** the variable-length intake raised output to 285 PS ([Porsche, 30 years of the 993](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/30-Jahre-911-Carrera-der-Generation-993-%E2%80%93-Der-letzte-seiner-Art.html); [Porsche 60-Years, innovations](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/4.-Generation---Karosserievarianten-und-Innovationen.html)).
- **Weight:** the coupé weighed 1,370 kg DIN (1,395 kg with Tiptronic), only about 20 kg more than the 964 coupé ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_993)).

**The family.**
- **911 Turbo (1995):** the first 911 with two turbochargers and the first Turbo with standard all-wheel drive, rated at 408 PS. Its 3.6-litre biturbo was claimed to have the lowest exhaust emissions of any series-production engine of its time, and it introduced hollow-spoke alloy wheels, a first in car manufacturing ([Porsche Newsroom 2018](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-4-type-993-16486.html); [Porsche 60-Years, innovations](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/4.-Generation---Karosserievarianten-und-Innovationen.html)). Output rose to 430 PS from MY1996 and 450 PS from MY1998 ([Porsche Classic 993 Turbo](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/993/993-turbo/)).
- **GT2:** rear-wheel drive and limited to 100 cars, with up to 450 PS ([Porsche Newsroom 2018](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-4-type-993-16486.html)).
- **Carrera RS:** 300 PS from 3.8 litres and about 100 kg lighter than a Carrera ([Porsche, 30 years of the 993](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/30-Jahre-911-Carrera-der-Generation-993-%E2%80%93-Der-letzte-seiner-Art.html)).
- **Targa (MY1996):** no longer a removable panel but a large electric glass roof that slides under the rear window, which meant the roll-over bar could go ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/993/993/)).
- **Carrera 4S and Carrera S:** the Turbo's wide body without the turbo or its wing; 6,948 and 3,714 built ([DE Wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_993)).
- **Turbo S (1998):** 450 PS; Porsche says 435 were built ([Porsche Newsroom: 993 Turbo S](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/Porsche-Heritage-Experience/Porsche-911-(Typ-993)-Turbo-S.html)).
- **Specials:** Porsche Exclusive built 14 Turbo Cabriolets ([Porsche Classic 993 Turbo](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/993/993-turbo/)). A one-off 993 Speedster was Porsche's present to F. A. Porsche for his 60th birthday on 11 December 1995 ([Stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-993-the-story/)).

**End of the air-cooled era.** Production of the 996 Carrera began in September 1997, but special 993 versions were built until March 1998 ([Stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-993-the-story/)). The final air-cooled 911 off the line was a Carrera 4S for Jerry Seinfeld, completed on 31 March 1998. A Turbo later nicknamed "The Last Waltz" came off the line on 27 March 1998, the day Ferry Porsche died. It then spent six months in the Sonderwunsch (special-request) department before delivery on 5 September 1998, so it was the last 993 to leave the factory ([Stuttcars, Last Waltz](https://www.stuttcars.com/1998-porsche-993-turbo-last-waltz/); [Classic Motorsports](https://classicmotorsports.com/cars-for-sale/the-last-waltz-final-air-cooled-porsche-911/)). Porsche counts 68,881 993s built up to the 1998 financial year ([Porsche 60-Years, gen 4](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/4.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(993),-1993-1998.html); [Porsche infographic](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:046b6519-d481-40b3-a2bd-f27ae911d354/05_911er_Generationen_993_EN.jpg)). Kittler's model-by-model count totals 68,029, including 23,127 Carrera Coupés ([EN Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(993))). "After 35 years of air-cooled models, a new era of water-cooled 911s would begin" ([Porsche, 30 years of the 993](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/30-Jahre-911-Carrera-der-Generation-993-%E2%80%93-Der-letzte-seiner-Art.html)).

## 11-996-1-1998 — 911 Carrera (996.1), the first water-cooled 911

### Why the 996 happened
By the early 1990s Porsche needed a cheaper-to-build 911 and a higher-volume entry model to survive. August Achleitner, then head of technical product planning and "strategic head of the overall 996 vehicle concept", recalls that "Porsche needed a car in a lower price segment, to help generate a higher volume of sales. So this led to the idea of the Boxster and the 996 sharing parts" ([Porsche Newsroom, 25 years of the 996](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html)). The engine question was open for a while: air-cooled two-valve engines were "at the end of the road technologically in terms of emissions and power", air-cooled four-valve boxers suffered hotspots, and "in 1989, a compact V8 was even fitted in the rear on a trial basis" before Porsche settled on water-cooled four-valve flat-sixes ([Porsche Newsroom, 25 years of the 996](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html)). Porsche's own history kit states the switch was forced by exhaust regulations: a four-valve head was needed, "which could only be cooled reliably in all operating situations with water" ([Porsche 60 Years 911 press kit, 5th generation](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/5.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(996),-1997-2005.html)). The shared platform meant the 996 and the 986 Boxster were identical from the front bumper to the B-pillars, including the interior up to that point ([Porsche Newsroom, 25 years of the 996](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html)). Wikipedia (citing Total 911) adds that Toyota consultants helped re-organise Zuffenhausen production and that parts-sharing cut development cost by about 30 % ([Wikipedia EN, Porsche 911 (996)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(996))) — secondary claims, not confirmed by a Porsche source in this session.

### Design and designers
Styling was led by Style Porsche under chief designer Harm Lagaaij (anglicised Lagaay), who headed the department from 1989; Porsche's 2004 retirement release says "the 996-series Porsche 911 and the Cayenne are also products of his pen" ([Porsche press release, 2004, via Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20131105201331/http://www.porsche.com/international/aboutporsche/pressreleases/archive2004/quarter2/?pool=international-de&id=press86); [Porsche Newsroom, One icon, four designers](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/one-icon-four-designers-10714.html)). The exterior is credited to studio chief Pinky Lai ([Wikipedia EN, Pinky Lai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinky_Lai); [Wikipedia EN, Porsche 911 (996)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(996))), and Porsche's own 996 feature says "Porsche designers Pinky Lai and Grant Larson" integrated the controversial headlights into the nose ([porsche.com, 25 years of 996: 7 facts](https://www.porsche.com/stories/mobility/25-years-of-996-7-facts/)). Lagaaij explains the Boxster look: the 1993 Detroit Boxster concept was a hit ("Best of Show"), and "I knew immediately that the show car's front end would fit with the 996 as well"; his team swelled to 80 people and modellers worked directly on 1:1 models ([Porsche Newsroom, 25 years of the 996](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html)).

What changed visually vs the 993: an all-new, smoother body, 185 mm longer and 30 mm wider, on a wheelbase stretched by 80 mm — only the second wheelbase stretch in 911 history ([Porsche Newsroom, seven generations part 5](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-5-typ-996-16487.html); [porsche.com, 7 facts](https://www.porsche.com/stories/mobility/25-years-of-996-7-facts/)). The headlight units combined five functions (low beam, high beam, indicator, parking light, fog light) in one cheap module that "could be installed in minutes", earning the nickname "fried eggs" ([Porsche Newsroom, 25 years of the 996](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html); [Wikipedia DE, Porsche 996](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_996)). The drag coefficient fell to Cd 0.30 ([Porsche 60 Years kit, 5th gen design](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/5.-Generation---Design-und-Modellvarianten.html)); the Carrera got an extending rear spoiler, the classic stepped wing-to-door contour disappeared and the full-width rear light band was dropped ([Wikipedia DE, Porsche 996](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_996)). The windscreen is about five degrees flatter ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-carrera-996-1998-2001/)). Inside, the five dials "merge into each other" — "a break with previous models" ([Porsche 60 Years kit, 5th gen design](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/5.-Generation---Design-und-Modellvarianten.html)). Porsche says that apart from the name and engine layout, only the bonnet crest, wheel-hub covers, steering wheel and airbags were carried over from the 993, and that the car weighed about 50 kg less thanks to high-strength steel ([Porsche 60 Years kit, 5th gen](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/5.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(996),-1997-2005.html)).

### Launch and the reference car
The 996 was shown at the IAA in Frankfurt in September 1997 ([Porsche Newsroom, 25 years of the 996](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html)); Wikipedia gives 17 July 1997 as production start ([Wikipedia EN, Porsche 911 (996)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(996))) and model year 1998 ("W-Programm") as the first model year ([Wikipedia DE, Porsche 996](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_996)). The MY1998 911 Carrera Coupé started at DM 135,610 ([Porsche 60 Years kit, 5th gen](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/5.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(996),-1997-2005.html)). Its M96 flat-six had 3,387 cc (96 × 78 mm) and made 300 PS (221 kW) at 6,800 rpm and 350 Nm at 4,600 rpm — the same output as the 1978 911 Turbo 3.3 ([Wikipedia DE, Porsche 996](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_996); [Porsche Newsroom, seven generations part 5](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-5-typ-996-16487.html)). With the standard six-speed manual it reached 100 km/h in 5.2 s and 280 km/h; a five-speed Tiptronic S was optional ([Wikipedia DE, Porsche 996](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_996); [Excellence specs, 1999 Carrera](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/57); [PCNA 2001 press kit](https://web.archive.org/web/20120916103006/http://press.porsche.com/archive/products/press_kits/press_kits_2001/PDF/1-4_FULL_LINE_RELEASE.pdf)).

### Variants during 996.1 (1998–2001)
- **Cabriolet** — April 1998, fully electric roof in 20 s ([Porsche Newsroom, 25 years](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html)).
- **Carrera 4** — about six months later (autumn 1998), same narrow body ([Porsche Newsroom, 25 years](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html)); the transmission tunnel had been sized for AWD from the start.
- **911 GT3** — unveiled at Geneva in March 1999 and built in Weissach from May 1999, 360 PS, a homologation special and "successor to the 911 Carrera RS"; the first car to wear the GT3 badge. Walter Röhrl lapped the Nordschleife in under eight minutes before its debut ([Porsche Newsroom, 20 years of the GT3](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2019/products/porsche-20-years-911-gt3-jubilee-six-generations-18311.html); [Porsche Newsroom, 25 years](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html); [porsche.com, 7 facts](https://www.porsche.com/stories/mobility/25-years-of-996-7-facts/)).
- **911 Turbo** — debut IAA September 1999 ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(996))), on sale January 2000 with a 420 PS twin-turbo engine derived from the Le Mans-winning GT1; the first series Porsche to exceed 300 km/h ([Porsche Newsroom, 25 years](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html); [Porsche 60 Years kit, 5th gen engines](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/5.-Generation---Motoren.html)).
- **911 GT2** — January 2001, 462 PS, the first model with ceramic brakes (PCCB) as standard ([Porsche Newsroom, 25 years](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html); [Porsche 60 Years kit, 5th gen bodystyles & innovations](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/5.-Generation---Karosserievarianten-und-Innovationen.html)).
- **Millennium Edition** (2000) — Carrera 4 in Violet Chromaflair, limited to 911 cars ([Wikipedia DE, Porsche 996](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_996)).

### Production
Porsche gives 175,262 cars for the whole 996 generation ([Porsche 60 Years kit, 5th gen](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/5.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(996),-1997-2005.html); [Porsche Newsroom, seven generations part 5](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-5-typ-996-16487.html)); stuttcars quotes 175,164 ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-996-sales-production-numbers/)). Calendar-year figures (Streather, via Wikipedia) of 14 (1997), 9,248 (1998), 28,040 (1999), 20,979 (2000) and 27,275 (2001) put roughly 85,556 cars in the 996.1 years, all body styles and models included ([Wikipedia EN, Porsche 911 (996)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(996))). Porsche says more than 30,000 996s were sold a year ([Porsche Newsroom, 25 years](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html)). Later criticism focused on M96 engine failures (intermediate-shaft bearing, bore scoring), which Wikipedia documents from aftermarket and lawsuit sources ([Wikipedia EN, Porsche 911 (996)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(996))).

## 12-996-2-2002 — 911 Carrera (996.2, model year 2002 update)

### Why the facelift
Within months of launch the 996's integrated headlight units "fell into disfavour with the press", which surprised the designers because the same lamp had been praised on the Boxster concept ([Porsche Newsroom, 25 years of the 996](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html)). Porsche's design history says chief designer Harm Lagaay "remedied this blemish by a facelift" ([Porsche Newsroom, One icon, four designers](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/one-icon-four-designers-10714.html)). With the mid-life update "the regular 996 was given the front headlights of the 911 Turbo model, which were rounded off at the bottom so as to differentiate it more clearly from the Boxster" ([Porsche 60 Years 911 press kit, 5th generation](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/5.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(996),-1997-2005.html)). Wikipedia (citing Porsche Club GB) says the change followed complaints from Carrera owners that a cheaper car looked just like theirs ([Wikipedia EN, Porsche 911 (996)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(996))).

### What changed (MY2002)
Porsche Cars North America's 2002 press kit lists the changes: a 3.6-litre engine replacing the 3.4, now 320 hp (from 300) with peak torque of 273 lb-ft at 4,250 rpm (from 258 lb-ft at 4,600), helped by VarioCam Plus variable timing and lift; "the headlight design of the 911 Turbo, plus a newly shaped front end, widened rear quarter panels, and redesigned oval exhaust tailpipes"; 15 % more airflow to the radiators and less lift at both axles; a stiffer body; new Carrera II 17-inch wheels; optional bi-xenon lights; a three-spoke steering wheel and a standard on-board computer ([PCNA 2002 Fast Facts](https://web.archive.org/web/20150324053349/http://press.porsche.com:80/archive/products/press_kits/press_kits_2002/PDF/34-35_CarreraFastFacts.pdf)). PCNA's "What's New for 2002" adds "redesigned front end, headlights and taillight sections for closer resemblance to 911 Turbo", a lockable glovebox, a cupholder in the dash and redesigned centre vents ([PCNA What's New 2002](https://web.archive.org/web/20151004063021/http://press.porsche.com/archive/products/press_kits/press_kits_2002/PDF/1-2_WhatsNew2002.pdf)). Porsche confirms that displacement rose to 3,596 cc and output to 320 PS for model year 2002 ([Porsche Newsroom, 25 years of the 996](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html)). Auto Bild's catalogue data puts the updated Carrera's sales start at 08/2001 ([Auto Bild, Porsche 911 996](https://www.autobild.de/marken-modelle/porsche/911/996/)).

Visually, the silhouette, glasshouse and wheelbase are unchanged from the 996.1. The differences are at the ends: clear Turbo-style headlamps whose lower edge is rounded off, a bumper with bigger air intakes, reworked tail-light sections and oval tailpipes ([PCNA 2002 Fast Facts](https://web.archive.org/web/20150324053349/http://press.porsche.com:80/archive/products/press_kits/press_kits_2002/PDF/34-35_CarreraFastFacts.pdf); [Porsche 60 Years kit, 5th gen](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/5.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(996),-1997-2005.html)). PCNA's 2004 spec sheet gives 4,430 × 1,770 × 1,305 mm, a 2,350 mm wheelbase and Cd 0.30 for the Carrera Coupé ([PCNA 2004 Carrera specs](https://web.archive.org/web/20160706001137/http://press.porsche.com:80/archive/products/press_kits/press_kits_2004/PDF/new/12-13_Carrera_Specs.pdf)).

### Reference car numbers
3,596 cc (96 × 82.8 mm), compression 11.3:1, 7,300 rpm limit, 315 hp SAE (PCNA's restated figure) or 320 PS (235 kW) at 6,800 rpm, 370 Nm at 4,250 rpm; six-speed manual or five-speed Tiptronic S; 0–100 km/h in 5.0 s (Tiptronic 5.5 s); 285 km/h (Tiptronic 280 km/h); curb weight 1,345 kg (Tiptronic 1,400 kg); 205/50 ZR17 and 255/40 ZR17 tyres on 7J and 9J × 17 wheels ([PCNA 2003 Carrera specs](https://web.archive.org/web/20150324053404/http://press.porsche.com:80/archive/products/press_kits/press_kits_2003/PDF/46-47_911_car_cp_cabspecs.pdf); [PCNA 2004 Carrera specs](https://web.archive.org/web/20160706001137/http://press.porsche.com:80/archive/products/press_kits/press_kits_2004/PDF/new/12-13_Carrera_Specs.pdf); [Wikipedia DE, Porsche 996](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_996); [Excellence specs, 2002 Carrera](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/63)). No verified German list price for the 996.2 coupé was found. Auto Bild/Schwacke lists €84,480 for the Carrera Cabriolet ([Auto Bild](https://www.autobild.de/marken-modelle/porsche/911/996/)).

### Model range growth
- **Targa** (MY2002): the sliding glass roof, now with a hinged rear window — "the first Porsche 911 model to feature a rear hatch opening" ([PCNA 2004 Carrera release](https://web.archive.org/web/20160706001026/http://press.porsche.com:80/archive/products/press_kits/press_kits_2004/PDF/new/1-11_Carrera_Release.pdf); [Porsche Newsroom, 25 years](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html)).
- **Carrera 4S Coupé** (MY2002, Cabriolet 2003) with the wider Turbo body ([Porsche Newsroom, 25 years](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html)). It brought back the full-width rear light band but kept neither the Turbo's fixed wing nor its rear-wing intakes ([Wikipedia DE, Porsche 996](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_996)). Wikipedia notes that 2001 was the last year of the narrow-body Carrera 4 Coupé ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(996))).
- **GT3 Mk II** (381 PS from 2003, thanks to VarioCam, with optional PCCB — [Porsche Newsroom, 20 years of the GT3](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2019/products/porsche-20-years-911-gt3-jubilee-six-generations-18311.html)) and **GT3 RS** (381 PS; 2003 per Porsche's history series, on sale 2004 per the 60-years kit) ([Porsche Newsroom, seven generations part 5](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-5-typ-996-16487.html); [Porsche 60 Years kit, 5th gen engines](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/5.-Generation---Motoren.html)). The GT2 rose to 483 PS ([Porsche Newsroom, seven generations part 5](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-5-typ-996-16487.html)).
- **"40 Jahre 911"** (MY2004): 345 PS, GT Silver paint, Turbo-style front cooling openings, polished 18-inch wheels, 1,963 units ([PCNA 2004 Carrera release](https://web.archive.org/web/20160706001026/http://press.porsche.com:80/archive/products/press_kits/press_kits_2004/PDF/new/1-11_Carrera_Release.pdf); [porsche.com, 7 facts](https://www.porsche.com/stories/mobility/25-years-of-996-7-facts/)).
- **Turbo Cabriolet** (MY2004) and **Turbo S** (MY2005, 450 PS) ([Porsche Newsroom, 25 years](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html)).

"Never before had there been so many variants of the 911 as in the 996 generation", and Porsche sold "roughly 175,000" ([Porsche Newsroom, 25 years](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-25-years-911-generation-996-30384.html)); the exact factory figure is 175,262 ([Porsche 60 Years kit, 5th gen](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/5.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(996),-1997-2005.html)). Calendar-year production for 2002–2005 was 33,013 + 29,536 + 23,145 + 4,012 = 89,706, all models ([Wikipedia EN, citing Streather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(996))). Pop culture: Sally Carrera in Pixar's *Cars* (2006) is a 996 designed to resemble a 2002 Carrera ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(996)); [porsche.com, 7 facts](https://www.porsche.com/stories/mobility/25-years-of-996-7-facts/)).

## 13-997-1-2005 — 911 Carrera (997.1)

### Development
European Car magazine's launch report (republished by MotorTrend) says work began in late 1998 with a group of 20 people from engineering, styling, pre-development and sales ([European Car / MotorTrend, 2005 Porsche 997 Carrera](https://www.motortrend.com/reviews/epcp-0411-2005-porsche-997-carrera/)). Wikipedia, citing the same magazine, dates the first body sketches to February 1999, clay models to 2000–2001, and says a final design by Grant Larson was approved ([Wikipedia EN, Porsche 911 (997)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(997))). Larson "had designed the Boxster concept and production cars, and supervised the appearance of the new Carrera GT"; his brief to himself was "to tighten everything up… to tighten the waist, to widen the hips", and drag fell from 0.30 to 0.28 ([European Car / MotorTrend](https://www.motortrend.com/reviews/epcp-0411-2005-porsche-997-carrera/)). The wider tyres in Larson's first sketches loaded the prototypes so heavily that they "literally destroyed the first prototypes in late 2001", forcing a wider redesign. Body rigidity rose by 8 % through welding and bonding techniques, and engineers claimed the car was 80 % changed from the 996, with the roof panel, rear seats and 3.6 block, crankshaft and pistons among the carry-overs ([European Car / MotorTrend](https://www.motortrend.com/reviews/epcp-0411-2005-porsche-997-carrera/)). The 997 was the last 911 overseen by chief designer Harm Lagaay, who "presented the type 997 shortly before he retired in 2004" ([Porsche Newsroom, One icon, four designers](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/one-icon-four-designers-10714.html)). He retired on 1 July 2004; Michael Mauer succeeded him no later than 1 November 2004 ([Porsche press release 2004, via Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20131105201331/http://www.porsche.com/international/aboutporsche/pressreleases/archive2004/quarter2/?pool=international-de&id=press86)).

### Launch
Porsche's history kit: "In July 2004, Porsche presented the sixth-generation 911 Carrera and 911 Carrera S, known internally as the 997" ([Porsche 60 Years 911 press kit, 6th generation](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/6.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(997),-2004-012.html)). The contemporary German press release adds the details. The international press drive was in June 2004. More than 100,000 customers were invited to premiere parties at all 85 German Porsche centres on Friday 16 July 2004, and every unveiling started at exactly 21:11 ("9.11 pm") under the motto "Präzision 911". The public open day and Europe-wide market launch (except right-hand-drive UK) followed on 17 July 2004 ([Porsche press release, July 2004, via Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20071023205602/http://www.porsche.com:80/germany/aboutporsche/pressreleases/archive2004/quarter3/?pool=germany&id=press62)). North American sales began on 28 August 2004 ([Porsche press release, Aug 2004, via Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20071023205602/http://www.porsche.com:80/germany/aboutporsche/pressreleases/archive2004/quarter3/?pool=germany&id=press60)). Prices were €75,200 in Germany including VAT for the Carrera and €85,176 for the Carrera S, with euro base prices of €64,700 and €73,300 ([Porsche press release, July 2004](https://web.archive.org/web/20071023205602/http://www.porsche.com:80/germany/aboutporsche/pressreleases/archive2004/quarter3/?pool=germany&id=press62)); US prices started at $69,300, "$700 more than the 996" ([European Car / MotorTrend](https://www.motortrend.com/reviews/epcp-0411-2005-porsche-997-carrera/)).

### Design changes vs the 996
- **Headlights:** "oval, clear-glass front headlights with auxiliary lights in the front end, which again recalled traditional 911 design cues", more steeply angled and "clearly differentiated… from the Boxster" ([Porsche 60 Years kit, 6th gen](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/6.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(997),-2004-012.html); [Porsche 60 Years kit, 6th gen design](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/6.-Generation---Design-und-Modellvarianten.html)).
- **Body:** "Apart from the roof, every visible body part of the 997 differed from that of the 996"; curved wings; a more muscular rear; the S, GT and Turbo versions 44 mm wider ([Porsche 60 Years kit, 6th gen](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/6.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(997),-2004-012.html); [Porsche Newsroom, seven generations part 6](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-6-type-997-16489.html)). Porsche's launch text cites a wider track and a more emphasised waist ([Porsche press release, July 2004](https://web.archive.org/web/20071023205602/http://www.porsche.com:80/germany/aboutporsche/pressreleases/archive2004/quarter3/?pool=germany&id=press62)); European Car gives +21 mm front and +34 mm rear track ([European Car / MotorTrend](https://www.motortrend.com/reviews/epcp-0411-2005-porsche-997-carrera/)).
- **Aero:** full underbody trim on all Carreras ([Porsche 60 Years kit, 6th gen](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/6.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(997),-2004-012.html)), giving Cd 0.28 for the Carrera ([porsche.com technical data 2008, via Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20080402125856/http://www.porsche.com:80/international/models/911/911-carrera/featuresandspecs/)).
- **Wheels and identifiers:** 18-inch wheels standard on the Carrera, 19-inch on the S; the S has four tailpipes and red calipers ([Wikipedia DE, Porsche 997](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_997); [PCNA 2005 specs](https://web.archive.org/web/20150122124455/http://press.porsche.com/archive/products/press_kits/press_kits_2005/pdf/carrera_carreras/911_carrera_specs.pdf)).
- **Interior:** almost all new except the rear seats ([European Car / MotorTrend](https://www.motortrend.com/reviews/epcp-0411-2005-porsche-997-carrera/)).

### Technology
PASM adaptive damping was standard on the Carrera S. The Sport Chrono Plus package changed throttle, PSM, PASM and Tiptronic mapping at the push of a button, and variable-ratio steering appeared ([Porsche 60 Years kit, 6th gen](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/6.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(997),-2004-012.html); [Porsche 60 Years kit, 6th gen bodystyles & innovations](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/6.-Generation---Karosserievarianten-und-Innovationen.html)); European Car calls the steering "Porsche's first for the 911" ([European Car / MotorTrend](https://www.motortrend.com/reviews/epcp-0411-2005-porsche-997-carrera/)). The Carrera's 3.6 made 325 PS and the new 3.8-litre Carrera S 355 PS, "the first 911 since 1977 to be offered with naturally aspirated engines with two different displacements" ([Porsche 60 Years kit, 6th gen engines](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/6.-Generation---Motoren.html)). The reference Carrera did 0–100 km/h in 5.0 s and 285 km/h, and weighed 1,395 kg DIN (1,470 kg EC) on a 4,427 × 1,808 × 1,310 mm body and 2,350 mm wheelbase ([porsche.com technical data 2008, via Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20080402125856/http://www.porsche.com:80/international/models/911/911-carrera/featuresandspecs/)).

## 14-997-2-2009 — 911 Carrera (997.2, model year 2009 facelift)

Porsche's German press release of 3 July 2008 is headlined "The new Porsche 911 goes on sale in Germany — More performance, less fuel consumption – under ten litres per 100 kilometers for the first time ever". It also says Porsche called the car "997 Gen II" internally, citing AutoWeek ([Wikipedia EN, Porsche 911 (997)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(997))).

### Technology: direct injection and PDK
"After the model update in autumn 2008, Porsche offered the 911 with petrol direct injection and an optional dual-clutch transmission (PDK)… With its 3.6-litre boxer engine the Carrera now delivered 345 PS, and the 3.8-litre managed 385 PS", with service intervals extended to 30,000 km ([Porsche 60 Years 911 press kit, 6th gen engines](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/6.-Generation---Motoren.html)). Porsche's history series says it "thoroughly revamped the engine range and introduced direct fuel injection for the first time", and that fuel use and emissions fell while output rose ([Porsche Newsroom, seven generations part 6](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-6-type-997-16489.html)). The launch release quantifies it: the PDK Carrera Coupé used 9.8 l/100 km and CO2 fell by up to 15 % ([Porsche press release, July 2008](https://web.archive.org/web/2008/http://www.porsche.com:80/international/aboutporsche/pressreleases/?pool=international-de&id=2008-07-03)). The seven-speed PDK, supplied by ZF according to DE Wikipedia, replaced the five-speed Tiptronic S. PDK cars now accelerated faster than their manual equivalents, which had previously been true only of the Tiptronic Turbo ([Wikipedia DE, Porsche 997](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_997)). The new engines were all-new designs rather than evolutions of the M96/M97 ([Wikipedia DE, Porsche 997](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_997)); Wikipedia's engine list codes them MA1.02 (3.6) and MA1.01 (3.8) ([Wikipedia EN, List of Porsche engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Porsche_engines)).

Bore × stroke is 97 × 81.5 mm, output peaks at 6,500 rpm, and the car runs 235/40 ZR18 and 265/40 ZR18 tyres on 8J and 10.5J × 18 wheels ([Wikipedia DE, Porsche 997](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_997); [Excellence specs, 2009 Carrera](https://www.excellence-mag.com/resources/specs/104)).

## 15-991-1-2012 — 911 Carrera (991.1), 2011/12–2015

- **Orders and launch:** orders opened on 1 September and the market launch began on **3 December 2011**.
- **German prices:** "88,038 euro for the 911 Carrera and 102,436 euro for the 911 Carrera S, including 19 per cent VAT" ([Porsche press release 84/11](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:05c3198c-772f-4e4d-88ad-b6fce31b66ed/S11_0327_en.pdf)). Porsche's 2023 retrospective quotes 88,037 euros for the 2012 model-year Carrera Coupé ([60 Years press kit, gen 7](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/7.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(991),-2011-2019.html)).

### A new platform
Porsche calls the 991 "a big technical jump": "Porsche further developed almost 90 per cent of the components, or redesigned them". The wheelbase grew by 10 cm and the tracks widened. The new aluminium-steel body saved 80 kg on the body itself. The interior "referenced the Carrera GT" ([60 Years, gen 7](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/7.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(991),-2011-2019.html)). The 2011 launch release adds:
- **Weight:** the new aluminium-steel body is "responsible for a significant proportion of the weight reduction of up to 45 kilograms".
- **Aero:** a "wider, variably extending rear spoiler" reduced lift.
- **Mirrors:** the exterior mirrors moved to "the upper edge of the door and not as before on the mirror triangle".
- **Drivetrain firsts:** "the world's first seven-speed manual transmission", electro-mechanical power steering, and PDCC active roll stabilisation "available for the first time on the 911 Carrera S".
- **Economy:** fuel use and emissions fell by up to 16 %. The Carrera PDK, at 194 g/km CO₂, was "the first Porsche sports car to make it below the 200 g/km mark".
([Press release 84/11](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:05c3198c-772f-4e4d-88ad-b6fce31b66ed/S11_0327_en.pdf))

When the last 991 was built, Porsche wrote that "it was the first time that a new 911 had weighed less than its predecessor" ([Newsroom, 20 Dec 2019](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2019/products/porsche-last-911-generation-991-19601.html)). Its "From zero to 1,000,000" review notes that the base engine shrank to 3.4 litres "yet still deliver[ed] 5 hp more than the 997/II" ([Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-seven-generations-13735.html)).

## 18-992-2-2024 — 911 Carrera (992.2), 2024–today

### T-Hybrid (Carrera GTS)
- **Engine:** a new 3.6-litre flat-six (bore 97 mm, stroke 81 mm) with a single electric exhaust-gas turbocharger. An electric motor sits between compressor and turbine; it spins the turbo up and generates up to 11 kW. There is no wastegate, and one turbo replaces the previous two ([Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2024/products/porsche-new-911-world-premiere-hybrid-36322.html)).
- **Hybrid hardware:** a permanent-magnet synchronous motor in the new eight-speed PDK adds up to 150 Nm and 40 kW. The battery is 400 V and 1.9 kWh gross, "corresponds in size and weight to a conventional 12-volt starter battery", and weighs about 27 kg ([Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2024/products/porsche-new-911-world-premiere-hybrid-36322.html); [EN press kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:baaedc84-1430-447f-9bd1-94636087e9bc/PAG_Pressemappe_911-EN.pdf)).
- **Output:** 357 kW (485 PS) and 570 Nm from the engine alone; **398 kW (541 PS) and 610 Nm** as a system. That is +61 PS over the old GTS, for only about +50 kg. 0–100 km/h takes 3.0 s and top speed is 312 km/h ([Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2024/products/porsche-new-911-world-premiere-hybrid-36322.html); [DE release](https://newsroom.porsche.com/de/2024/produkte/porsche-neuer-911-weltpremiere-hybrid-36321.html)).

### Design leadership
Michael Mauer was still "Vice President Style Porsche" when the 992.2 Spirit 70 launched in April 2025 ([Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2025/products/porsche-911-spirit-70-39294.html)). On 28 Jan 2026 Porsche announced that **Tobias Sühlmann** (from McLaren) would succeed him as Head of Design from **1 February 2026**. The same release confirms that Mauer began in **2004** as "the fourth Head of Design in Porsche's long history", after **Ferdinand Alexander Porsche, Anatol Lapine and Harm Lagaay** ([Newsroom, 28 Jan 2026](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2026/company/porsche-head-of-design-tobias-suehlmann-michael-mauer-41611.html)). Any 911 facelift or successor after 2026 will therefore be the first under a new design chief. That is a useful closing line for the timeline.

## Where the 911 stands in September 2026

**No successor has been revealed.** As of 27 September 2026 the current car is still the 992.2, launched on 28 May 2024 ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2024/products/porsche-new-911-world-premiere-hybrid-36322.html)). Porsche's English news sitemap for 2026, with articles up to late September, contains no new-generation 911 and no second facelift. Every 911 product release in 2026 is a 992.2 derivative or a one-off ([Newsroom 2026 sitemap](https://newsroom.porsche.com/sitemap-en-news-2026.xml)). The most recent 911 releases are:

- **7 September 2025, 911 Turbo S (T-Hybrid).** Shown at the IAA Mobility in Munich. System output is 523 kW (711 PS) with 800 Nm, making it "the most powerful production 911 to date" ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2025/products/porsche-911-turbo-s-top-of-the-range-model-twin-turbo-performance-hybrid-40446.html)).
- **14 April 2026, 911 GT3 S/C.** A GT3 with a fully automatic convertible roof: 510 PS, manual gearbox only, 1,497 kg ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2026/products/porsche-gt3-sc-world-premiere-lightweight-cabriolet-42136.html)).
- **August 2026, Monterey Car Week.** Porsche introduced the 911 Carrera S with MT Package, which brings a six-speed manual back to the 480 PS Carrera S for the US and Canada only. It also showed the Flachbau RS, a one-off by the Sonderwunsch department and Manthey that turns a 991.2 GT2 RS into a modern "slantnose" homage to the 935/78 "Moby Dick" ([Porsche Newsroom, 19 Aug 2026](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2026/products/porsche-flachbau-rs-monterey-car-week-2026-43079.html); [Porsche Newsroom US](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en_US/2026/products/in-the-slipstream-of-moby-dick-an-extraordinary-one-off-flachbau-model.html)).
- **26 August 2026, 911 Challenge.** A new entry-level customer racing car ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2026/motorsports/porsche-911-challenge-customer-motorsport-43118.html)).

**New people at the top.** Dr. Michael Leiters became CEO of Porsche AG on 1 January 2026 ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2025/company/porsche-dr-michael-leiters-becomes-ceo-on-1-january-2026-40864.html)). A month later Tobias Sühlmann succeeded Michael Mauer as head of design; he is only the fifth person to hold the job, after F. A. Porsche, Lapine, Lagaay and Mauer ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2026/company/porsche-head-of-design-tobias-suehlmann-michael-mauer-41611.html)). Whoever shapes the next 911 will be working under a new design chief.

**Demand.** In 2025 Porsche delivered 51,583 911s, 1 per cent more than the year before and another delivery record for the model ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2026/company/porsche-deliveries-2025-41516.html)).

### 2. Stops that barely change shape, if the list has to shrink

The pairs below share a body shell or nearly so. The morph between them will be subtle, and a side-panel story will carry more than the 3D change. They are ranked from least to most visible difference:

1. **01 → 02 (901 show car → 911 2.0).** Details only: no bumper overriders or sill trim, twin tailpipes, and a 2,204 mm wheelbase on the prototype, which grew by 7 mm to 2,211 mm for production ([stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-f-series-the-story/)). Merge them, or reuse the 02 geometry with a "901" badge and the yellow of the IAA show car.
2. **07 → 08 (SC → Carrera 3.2).** Fog lamps set into the front apron ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(classic)); [Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/a15142185/1984-porsche-911-carrera-archived-road-test-review/)). From MY1987 there is a red reflective rear strip with built-in fog lights, and 16-inch wheels became standard from MY1989 ([Porsche Classic](https://www.porsche.com/international/accessoriesandservice/classic/models/911-g/911-carrera/)). The best merge candidate if a stop must go: "911 SC / Carrera 3.2, 1977–1989".
3. **13 → 14 (997.1 → 997.2).** Same body. Only the LED daytime running lights, the intakes, the LED tail lights and the mirrors change (see stop 14).
4. **15 → 16 (991.1 → 991.2).** Four-point lights, a louvred engine lid and new door handles (see stop 16).
5. **11 → 12 (996.1 → 996.2).** The Turbo-style headlights replace the "fried eggs" ([Porsche 60-years kit, gen 5](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/5.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(996),-1997-2005.html)); the intakes and tailpipes also change (see stop 12). Keep this one: the fix is a famous story and easy to show.
6. **17 → 18 (992.1 → 992.2).** No separate front driving lights, new intakes, and a light bar with the PORSCHE lettering integrated ([992.2 press kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:baaedc84-1430-447f-9bd1-94636087e9bc/PAG_Pressemappe_911-EN.pdf)).

The largest shape changes, and therefore the strongest morphs, are 00 → 01 (four-seat notchback to fastback), 03 → 04 (flares and ducktail), 04/05 (impact bumpers), 05 → 06 (Turbo wide body and whale tail), 08 → 09 (integrated bumpers and pop-up spoiler), 09 → 10 (flat-lamp front, wide hips), 10 → 11 (all-new water-cooled body) and 14 → 15 (100 mm longer wheelbase).

### 3. Which car to model at each stop (spec data stays on the base coupé)

- **Stop 05 makes the silhouette zig-zag.** The base 150 PS G-series coupé has narrow arches and no spoiler, so the car goes wide with a ducktail (04), narrow and plain (05), then wide with a whale tail (06). That is historically honest; label it, or model the Carrera 2.7 for visual continuity ([Porsche 60-years kit, gen 2](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/2.-Generation-911,-(G-Serie),-1973---1989.html)).
- **Stop 06 should show the 3.0 "whale tail" of 1975.** From MY1978 the 3.3 Turbo placed a charge-air cooler below its rear spoiler, which is why that later wing is the taller "tea-tray" shape ([Porsche Turbo press kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/50-years-porsche-turbo/The-911-Turbo-generations.html); see stop 06). The Turbo also overlaps 07 and 08 in time, from 1975 to 1989, so present it as a branch or label its full span.
- **Stop 03: the 911 T spec car looks almost identical to stop 02.** The visible differences are the rear wheel sitting 57 mm further back, slight flares and wider rims ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-seven-generations-part-1-original-911-16452.html)). For a clearer long-wheelbase look, model a 1972–73 911 S 2.4 with its front spoiler and Fuchs wheels.

### Stop-by-stop notes from the research

The assessments below were written by the researchers for each stop and are kept unedited. Where an assessment has no inline link, its facts are sourced in the matching stop section above; a link for its dates is added.

- **00-t7-1959.** **Keep it, as an optional prologue.** The shape is clearly different from every production 911 – a longer 2,400 mm wheelbase, a four-seat notchback/wraparound-glass roof and a flat tail – yet the nose is recognisably a 911, which makes a good first morph. Enough photos exist. **Label:** "1959 (design) / 1960 (prototype)". Porsche's own texts give 1959 and 1960, AutoBild and Wikipedia say 1961, and one caption says 1961/62. Keep the id `00-t7-1959`. **Naming:** use "Type 754 T7". The number "695" appears only in secondary sources and is attached to different things (the T7 itself, or a Goertz study), so avoid it in the UI. **Data caveat:** there is no factory specification. The data box describes the car with its Carrera 2 engine, and weight, track and Cd are unknown, so the side panel should say "prototype – approximate figures". ([dates source](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-one-millionth-911-milestone-automotive-icon-sports-car-13737.html); visual details are sourced in the 00-t7-1959 section above.)
- **01-901-1963.** **Years:** correct. The premiere was on 12 September 1963 (Porsche). Production of 901-badged cars ran from September to November 1964. **Visual distinctness:** very low. The 901 is essentially the production 911 short-wheelbase car (stop 02). The differences are details only: no bumper overriders or sill trim, a round fuel flap on the prototypes, twin tailpipes, no slanted "911" script, fixed rear quarter windows on the prototypes, and a wheelbase of 2,204 mm instead of 2,211 mm. **Recommendation:** either merge 01 into 02 as one "901/911 (1963–1968)" stop, or keep 01 as a narrative marker that reuses the stop-02 geometry with the detail deltas above. It could default to yellow paint (the IAA car) and badge text "901". **No missing milestone for this era.** The 912 (1965) and the Targa (shown in 1965, sold from MY1967) belong to stop 02's narrative, not to separate stops. ([dates source](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/history/porsche-911-evolutionary-history-754-901-ferry-porsche-14641.html); visual details are sourced in the 01-901-1963 section above.)
- **02-911-swb-1964.** **Years:** "1964–1968" is correct. Production ran from 14 Sept 1964 until the A-series ended in summer 1968 (model years 1965–1968). **Visual distinctness:** the stop is distinct from 03 mainly through the 57 mm shorter wheelbase, which puts the rear wheel visibly further forward in the arch. It also has narrow 4½J wheels with hubcaps and unflared arches. **Possible variant skin:** the 911 S with Fuchs wheels (MY1967) could be a wheel/trim variant rather than a separate stop. **Targa:** the Targa (1965/67) matters historically, but it is not a coupé, so it belongs in the side panel rather than as a stop. ([dates source](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/1.-Generation-Porsche-911-(Ur-Elfer),-1963-1973.html); visual details are sourced in the 02-911-swb-1964 section above.)
- **03-911-lwb-1969.** **Years:** the stop years are right if read as model years 1969–1973. Production ran from about August/September 1968 to mid-1973, and the G-series started with MY1974. **Visual distinctness:** on a scrubber the change from 02 is subtle: wheel position, slight arch flares and wider rims. It is real, though, and it is the only change to the original body's proportions. **Overlap with 04:** the stop overlaps stop 04, since the Carrera RS 2.7 is itself an F-series (MY1973) car. The chronological order 03 → 04 → 05 still works. **Reference model:** for a more recognisable "LWB look", consider modelling the 911 S 2.4 (1972–73: 6J Fuchs-style wheels, front spoiler, black trim) as the visual reference while keeping the 911 T as the spec reference. No additional stop is needed. The 2.2 and 2.4 steps are engine changes, not body changes. ([dates source](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-t-2-0-1968-1969/); visual details are sourced in the 03-911-lwb-1969 section above.)
- **04-carrera-rs-27-1973.** **Years are right.** The car was unveiled on 5 October 1972 and built from November 1972 to July 1973 as a 1973 model-year (F-series) car ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/history/porsche-50-years-911-carrera-rs-2-7-germanys-fastest-sports-car-28486.html); [stuttcars](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-carrera-rs-2-7/)). The id suffix "1973" is fine, though the debut year was 1972. **It is visually distinct and deserves its own stop.** The ducktail, flared rear arches, front spoiler and Carrera script clearly separate it from stop 03. **It overlaps stop 03 in time.** The RS is a variant of the last long-wheelbase series, so the scrubber shows a performance special rather than a new generation. That is worth labelling in the UI. **No extra stop needed.** The RS 3.0 and RSR are not needed as separate stops. **Pick one Touring/Sport data set.** For the reference coupé, use the Touring (1,075 kg, 6.3 s, 240 km/h), or show the Sport (960 kg, 5.8 s, 245 km/h) and label it clearly. The specs.json file uses Touring values.
- **05-g-series-1974.** **Years are right.** Stop 05 should cover the narrow-body 2.7 era, production August 1973 to July/August 1977 (MY1974–77). "1974–1977" matches Porsche's own dating ([Porsche Newsroom](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html)). **Keep it as a stop.** The impact bumpers are the single biggest visual change in the 911's first 25 years. **Watch the reference car.** The base 150 PS coupé is narrow-bodied, has no spoiler and runs on steel wheels. If the 3D team morphs from the RS 2.7 (stop 04), the arches will narrow and the ducktail disappear before stop 06 adds the Turbo's wide body. Consider showing a Carrera 2.7 (wide arches, ducktail/whale tail) instead, or accept the narrow base car and label it. **No missing milestone for this span.** Targa (1965), Sportomatic (1967) and galvanising (1975) are not coupé-shape changes. The Carrera 3.0 (1976) looks almost identical to the Carrera 2.7.
- **06-930-turbo-1975.** The dates are right: debut in October 1974, deliveries from spring 1975, production through MY1989. This is the **most visually distinct stop of the G-series era** (wide arches and whale tail), so it deserves its own stop. Two caveats: 1. The Turbo **overlaps** stops 07 and 08. On a linear scrubber the car would go narrow (05), then wide (06), then medium (07/08). Consider presenting 06 as a branch/overlay of the G-series, or clearly label it "1975–1989". 2. For a 1975 reference, model the **3.0 "whale tail"** (smooth, rubber-edged). The MY1978+ 3.3 has the taller "tea-tray" wing with raised side lips around the intercooler. Pick one and say which. The Slantnose (from 1983) and the 1987 Turbo Cabriolet are good side-panel facts rather than extra stops. ([dates source](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/50-Jahre-Porsche-911-(G-Serie)-%E2%80%93-Der-Dauerbrenner-von-Porsche.html); visual details are sourced in the 06-930-turbo-1975 section above.)

Visually, though, it is only modestly different from stop 05 (wider rear arches, black trim) and **almost identical to stop 08**. Recommendation: either **merge 07 + 08 into one "911 SC / Carrera 3.2, 1978–1989" stop**, or keep both but give the side panel the story (the Schutz decision, the first Cabriolet) rather than expecting a visible morph. The Cabriolet (MY1983) and the Speedster (1989) are milestones that suit the side panel better than their own coupé-based stops. ([dates source](https://www.stuttcars.com/porsche-911-sc-g-series-1978-1983/); visual details are sourced in the 07-911-sc-1978 section above.)
- **08-carrera-32-1984.** The years are correct: MY1984–1989, built from September 1983 to July 1989. Visually this stop is **nearly indistinguishable from stop 07 (SC)**. The morphing car would change only in its fog lamps, Fuchs wheels and, from MY1987, the rear reflector with fog lights. Options: 1. **Merge 07 and 08** into a single "911 SC / Carrera 3.2 (1978–1989)" stop and put the 1981 "911 saved" story and the 3.2's Motronic engine in the side panel. 2. Keep both, but treat 08 mainly as a data/story stop (and possibly default the paint selector to a period colour), since the morph from 07 will be subtle. If a G-series milestone were to be added, the most visually distinct candidate is the **1989 Speedster** (low windscreen, humped cover), but it is not a coupé. It fits better as a side-panel variant. ([dates source](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/2.-Generation---Design-und-Modellvarianten.html); visual details are sourced in the 08-carrera-32-1984 section above.)

- **11-996-1-1998.** Years 1998–2001 are right for the stop: production began in July 1997 and the IAA debut was in September 1997, but the car was sold as MY1998–MY2001. It is visually the most distinct break in 911 history (new body, fried-egg lamps, smoother flanks, longer wheelbase), so it clearly deserves its own stop. There is no missing milestone that needs a separate coupé stop: the 996 GT3 (1999) and 996 Turbo (2000) belong in notable_variants. ([dates source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(996)); visual details are sourced in the 11-996-1-1998 section above.)
- **12-996-2-2002.** The years (2002–2004) are right for the Carrera Coupé: production from August 2001 as MY2002, replaced by the 997 in July 2004. The body shell is identical to stop 11, so the visible difference is limited to the headlamp shape (clear, teardrop lower edge instead of the amber-cornered "fried egg"), the bumper intakes and the tailpipes. That is recognisable but subtle. Keep the stop if the headlight morph is modelled, because it is a well-known story ("fixing the fried eggs"); otherwise it is the best candidate to merge into stop 11. ([dates source](https://www.autobild.de/marken-modelle/porsche/911/996/); visual details are sourced in the 12-996-2-2002 section above.)
- **13-997-1-2005.** Id "13-997-1-2005" is fine (MY2005), but the car was revealed and on sale in July 2004, so the scrubber label could read "2004". It is visually distinct from the 996.2: round headlights, separate front auxiliary lamps, wider hips and larger wheels. Keep it. ([dates source](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/60-Years-Porsche-911/6.-Generation-Porsche-911,-(997),-2004-012.html); visual details are sourced in the 13-997-1-2005 section above.)
- **14-997-2-2009.** The years (2009–2012) are right as model years; the car was on sale from July 2008 and was replaced by the 991 from late 2011. Visually this is the least distinct stop in the 996/997 run: same body, glass and wheelbase as stop 13, with only LED running lights, reshaped intakes, LED tail lights and bigger mirrors to tell them apart. The technical milestones (first direct injection, first PDK) are important for the side panel, though. If the stop list must shrink, merge 13 and 14 into a single "997" stop and use the 997.2 data panel. If it stays, the morph should focus on bumper intakes, lamps and tail lights. ([dates source](https://web.archive.org/web/2008/http://www.porsche.com:80/international/aboutporsche/pressreleases/?pool=international-de&id=2008-07-03); visual details are sourced in the 14-997-2-2009 section above.)
- **15-991-1-2012.** Keep. Use "2012–2015" (IAA premiere Sept 2011, sales from Dec 2011, replaced by the 991.2 from Dec 2015). The change from the 997 is distinct and modelable: longer wheelbase, lower roof, door-mounted mirrors, very slim LED tail lights and a wider rear spoiler. ([dates source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_(991)); visual details are sourced in the 15-991-1-2012 section above.)
- **16-991-2-2016.** Keep, but flag that the visual change from stop 15 is small: four-point DRLs and brake lights, a louvred engine lid, new door handles and bumpers. The technical change (first turbocharged base Carrera) and the one-millionth-911 milestone (May 2017) justify a separate stop for the side panel; for the 3D morph, 15→16 will be a subtle step. Use "2016–2019" for the dates (German launch 12 Dec 2015; US MY2017). If the team wants fewer stops, 15 and 16 are the strongest candidates to merge into a single "991" stop, with 991.2 details in the panel. ([dates source](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:aed99995-b38a-42ad-846b-878cbd249c6f/~6042931.pdf); visual details are sourced in the 16-991-2-2016 section above.)
- **17-992-1-2019.** Keep. Label the dates "2019–2024", noting the premiere was Nov 2018 (Carrera S) and the base Carrera arrived July 2019. The step from the 991.2 is clearly visible: the wide body is standard, the rear is wider with a full-width light bar, and the bonnet has a recess. For the base-car morph, note that the 992.1 still had auxiliary front lights in the bumper. The 992.2 removed them because "all frontal lighting functions" moved into the headlights ([992.2 EN press kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:baaedc84-1430-447f-9bd1-94636087e9bc/PAG_Pressemappe_911-EN.pdf)).
- **18-992-2-2024.** **Keep this stop.** "2024–today" is correct (premiere 28 May 2024). **Visually distinct from 17:** the front has no separate driving lights and larger intakes; the rear has a new light bar with "PORSCHE" in the arc, five-fin grilles and a higher number plate. These are subtle but modelable. **No additional stop is needed for post-992.2 news:** nothing newer than the 992.2 has been revealed. **Optional:** a scrubber end-note that design chief Mauer handed over to Tobias Sühlmann on 1 Feb 2026, i.e. "the next 911 is still to come". ([dates source](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2024/products/porsche-new-911-world-premiere-hybrid-36322.html); visual details are sourced in the 18-992-2-2024 section above.)
